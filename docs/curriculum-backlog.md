# Kinetic curriculum backlog (second pass)

This is the backlog of candidate topics for the second pass of the Kinetic curriculum: everything a 4-year computer science degree covers, plus the harder material engineers pick up on the job as they move from junior to staff and principal. Nothing here is in `src/core/curriculum.ts` yet. Each entry is a proposal, with a one-line reason and a visual idea for the lesson.

**How it was produced.** Five surveyors each covered the field from a different angle and proposed topics. A synthesizer merged the roughly 320 proposals, removing duplicates, fixing tiers and grouping the topics into tracks. A completeness critic then checked the merged list against ACM/IEEE CS2023, a typical degree, an engineering ladder, the usual system-design interview topics and common outage causes, and added what was missing. No web search was used.

**Totals:** 406 topics (295 from the merge, 111 added by the critic). 4 topics carry a tier-correction note.

## New tracks

These seven tracks are proposed in addition to the seven existing ones (`dsa`, `net`, `arch`, `data`, `os`, `sec`, `ops`).

| id | Name | Topics | Rationale |
| --- | --- | ---: | --- |
| `hw` | Computer Architecture & Data Representation | 22 | Number representation (binary, two's complement, floats, text encoding), logic gates, the CPU, pipelines, caches, SIMD and storage devices make up a full CS-degree course. The os track covers the kernel, not the hardware underneath it. I merged in the number-representation topics from the math lens so all bit-level material lives in one place. |
| `lang` | Languages, Compilers & Theory of Computation | 33 | I merged the proposed 'theory' and 'lang' tracks. Automata lead to regex, then grammars, parsers and ASTs, then types, interpreters and JIT, then GC. That is one continuous story. Computability and complexity (Turing machines, halting, P vs NP, reductions) finish the same CS2023 foundations area. Keeping two thin tracks would have split parsing across both. |
| `math` | Math for CS | 35 | Discrete math, number theory, probability and statistics, linear algebra, and information and coding theory are roughly a third of a degree's required coursework. None of the existing tracks covers them. Applied topics like percentiles, A/B tests and queueing theory give them clear on-the-job value. |
| `craft` | Software Craft & Engineering Practice | 27 | Testing, debugging, design principles and patterns, refactoring, code review and dependency management are most of what engineers learn on the job from junior to senior. None of the technical tracks covers them. |
| `web` | Web, Graphics & UX | 25 | I merged the proposed 'web' and 'gfx' tracks. Browser rendering, DOM, CSS layout, web vitals, rendering strategies, mobile and offline clients, accessibility and HCI laws, plus the rasterization and GPU pipeline, together cover the client and visual side. The existing backend-heavy tracks leave it out entirely. |
| `ml` | ML & AI Systems | 21 | ML fundamentals (supervised learning, metrics, gradient descent, backprop, attention) are a standard degree area. Embeddings, RAG, LLM serving, evals and feature stores are now core engineering knowledge. No existing track fits them. |
| `lead` | Technical Leadership | 17 | Design docs, estimation, tech-debt strategy, large migrations, build vs buy, Conway's law, decision-making, mentoring and technical strategy define the staff and principal tiers. They fit no technical track. |

## How to promote a topic

1. Add an entry to `TOPICS` in `src/core/curriculum.ts` with its `id`, `tier`, `track`, `title` and `tagline`.
2. If the topic uses one of the new tracks, add that track to `TRACKS` in the same file first, and add its id to the `TrackId` type in `src/core/types.ts`.
3. Build the lesson at `src/lessons/<id>/`. The topic becomes playable once `src/lessons/<id>/index.tsx` exists. Until then it shows as "planned".
4. Remove the topic from this backlog, or mark it as promoted.

> The backlog is already very strong on DSA, theory, math, distributed systems and the database, OS and security basics. The main gaps are in five areas:
>
> 1. **Society, Ethics & Professionalism.** This CS2023 area was almost completely missing. I added licensing, ethics and ML fairness, all placed in the existing lead and ml tracks.
> 2. **Everyday programming-language constructs.** Error handling, async/await, generics, iterators and Option types were absent.
> 3. **Topics that come from real incidents.** Timeouts and deadlines, cache stampedes, poison messages, config rollouts, resource exhaustion, certificate expiry and time-zone bugs were missing. These cause a large share of actual outages.
> 4. **Common system-design interview topics.** ID generation, fan-out, multi-tenancy, job scheduling, ledgers, heavy hitters and adaptive streaming were not covered.
> 5. **Staff-level skills that aren't technical.** Influence without authority, prioritization, critical-path planning, technical writing and product thinking fill out the lead track.
>
> Smaller gaps:
> - **Computer architecture:** ISA and assembly, calling conventions, sequential logic, endianness.
> - **Graphics:** Beziers, projection, ray tracing, colour.
> - **Frontend:** bundlers, storage, i18n, responsive design, state management.
> - **Classic ML:** regression, trees, clustering, RL, CNNs, tokenization, agents.
>
> I found no new track necessary. Everything fits into the existing tracks plus the proposed ones. The tier corrections mainly move a few overly senior topics (Bloom filters, LSM trees, observability, load balancing, OAuth, RSA, gradient descent) down so prerequisites come before the topics that depend on them.
