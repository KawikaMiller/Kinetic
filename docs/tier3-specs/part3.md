**`kafka-partitioned-log` · Partitioned Logs: Kafka Internals** **[domain review]**
Track `data` · *"Partitions, offsets and consumer groups"* · Builds on: `message-queues`
Opens by quoting `poison-messages-dlq`'s Out of scope ("partitioned logs (T3 `kafka-partitioned-log`)") and `message-queues`' ("broker basics, acks and at-least-once delivery"): this lesson starts at the *log*, not the queue.
**Spec.**
- **Scene 1 · Partitions and keys.** A topic with P partitions (1 to 6) drawn as P horizontal tapes of offset cells (`CellGrid`, one row per partition, `rowDecor` rail for the leader broker). A producer panel sends records: the learner types a key (or picks a key set: 5 user ids, 1 hot key, no key) and presses Send, or runs a seeded burst of 40 records.
  - A keyed record goes to `hash(key) mod P`; a keyless record goes round-robin (stated as "this lesson's rule"). The hash is `core/hash` FNV-1a and the chip says **"Kafka's default partitioner uses murmur2 on the key bytes (verify against the Kafka docs); the placement shown here is FNV-1a, so real partition numbers differ"**.
  - The offset is the position within *one* partition. A "global order" strip shows arrival order across partitions beside the per-partition order, so the learner sees that order exists inside a tape only.
  - A **P slider after data exists** repartitions: keys remap by `hash mod P`, and a counter shows how many keys now live in a different partition than before (computed from the real hash, expected about P/(P+1) of keys when going P to P+1). The caption says per-key order across the change is not preserved.
- **Scene 2 · Consumer groups and offsets.** Group A has C consumers (1 to 8) over P partitions; assignment is round-robin by partition index (stated as one assignor; "range" is named only).
  - Each partition has a **log-end offset**, a **committed offset** and the consumer's **position**; lag is `log-end - committed`, drawn as a bar per partition. Consumers beyond P sit idle and the stage says so ("at most one consumer per partition in a group").
  - A second group B reads the same log independently from its own offsets.
  - A **commit policy** toggle: commit *after* processing (at-least-once) or *before* (at-most-once). Pressing **Crash consumer** while a record is processed makes the replacement resume from the committed offset: the learner sees a record processed twice, or skipped, and the processed-count ledger shows it.
  - **Rebalance:** adding or removing a consumer reassigns partitions; the `LogStream` shows "revoke P2 from c1, assign P2 to c3" lines, and a **stop-the-world** pause bar (a named constant, labelled illustrative, because real pauses depend on protocol and config; verify against the Kafka docs for cooperative rebalancing).
- **Scene 3 · Replication and the high watermark** (K3-8). One partition replicated across 3 brokers (leader plus two followers) on `createCluster`. Controls: `acks` (0, 1, all), `min.insync.replicas` (1, 2), **unclean leader election** on/off, and fault buttons (crash leader, delay a follower, restart).
  - The ISR set, each replica's log-end offset and the **high watermark** (the highest offset every ISR member has) are drawn on three rows; consumers only read up to the high watermark.
  - With `acks=1`, the producer is acknowledged when the leader has the record; crashing the leader before a follower copied it and electing that follower loses an acknowledged record, and the lost record is drawn in `--bad` with its offset.
  - With `acks=all` and `min.insync.replicas=2` the write waits for the ISR, and the same fault schedule loses nothing; with fewer replicas in the ISR than `min.insync.replicas` the producer gets a refusal (availability traded for durability, shown).
  - With unclean election on, a replica outside the ISR can become leader and discard committed data.
  - Defaults are hedged on screen: "`acks=all` and idempotence are the producer defaults in recent Kafka versions; `min.insync.replicas` defaults to 1; unclean election is off by default (verify against the Kafka docs for your version)".
- **Scene 4 · Retention and compaction.** Two policies on one partition: *time/size retention* deletes whole old segments (the base offset moves; a consumer whose position fell below it gets "offset out of range"), and *log compaction* keeps the latest record per key and drops older ones (a tombstone, a null value, deletes the key). The learner picks the policy and appends updates to three keys; the compacted log is checked against a `Map` replay.
**Out of scope.** Exactly-once and transactions (T3 `exactly-once-myth`; named only), stream processing on top (`stream-windowing`), Raft and the KRaft controller (the registered `raft` lesson; named in words), the wire protocol, Kafka Streams and Connect, schema registries (`schema-evolution`).
**Kit.** `CellGrid` with K3-16 `rowDecor` (partition rows, leader rail), `core/distSim` (K3-8: `createCluster`, `checkInvariants`, `forSeeds`, `toSequence`), `SequenceDiagram` for one replication round, `LogStream` (K3-9), `Bar` (lag), `Segmented`, `Slider`, `Toggle`, `ChipButton` presets, `core/hash`, `core/random`, `Provenance` (K3-7), `useScenePicker`.
**AC.**
- **Watch:** records are tones by key (categorical, never `--bad`/`--ok`); the committed offset is `--ok`, the consumer position `--info`, lag `--violet`; a lost acknowledged record and a refused write are `--bad`; the high watermark and the record in flight are `--hi`; an idle consumer and a deleted segment are `--dim`; partition-leader rails are `--violet`.
- **Misconception:** "Kafka keeps all messages in order" (order is per partition) and "more consumers always means faster" (capped at P). Two quiz questions target them; a third asks which `acks` setting can lose an acknowledged write.
- **Correctness:**
  - Placement: a seeded 1,000-key set lands in each partition within a stated tolerance of 1/P, and the same key always lands in one partition (property over 10,000 draws).
  - Group assignment: every partition has exactly one owner for every (C, P) in 1..8 × 1..6, with `min(C, P)` busy consumers (exhaustive); a rebalance changes only ownership, never offsets.
  - At-least-once: for 200 crash schedules with a commit after (or before) every record, the commit-after policy loses no record and duplicates at most the in-flight one; commit-before never duplicates and loses at most one (invariants asserted, with the counterexample trace).
  - **Invariant for rule 20 (safety, checked every step over N >= 200 seeds with delay, loss, duplication and leader crash):** *no record acknowledged under `acks=all` with the ISR at least `min.insync.replicas` is ever missing from the leader that is elected afterwards*, and the high watermark never decreases on a live leader. **Broken mode:** `acks=1` with unclean election on. A `forSeeds` test must report the lowest violating seed; that seed is pinned in the test and used as the default preset. The builder also pins a hand-built schedule (append, ack, crash the leader before the follower fetch) that violates it on every seed, so the lesson never depends on luck. If no seed in 1..200 violates the broken mode, the scenario is wrong and is fixed, not staged.
  - Compaction output equals "replay into a `Map`" for 200 seeded logs, tombstones included. Retention deletes only whole segments (base offset is a segment boundary).
  - Liveness is stated separately: a minority of brokers makes `acks=all` writes wait or be refused; nothing claims progress.
- **Hostile input:** a key longer than 40 characters is truncated for display and hashed whole; an empty key is the keyless case, said so; P and C are clamped by `parseBoundedInt` with a refusal message; a burst over 200 records is refused; no wall-clock enters the model.
- **Honesty:** `Provenance` chips: "simulated, seed N" on scenes 1 to 4; "illustrative" on the pause bar; "from memory, verify against the Kafka docs" next to the defaults, the partitioner and cooperative rebalancing. The stage says it models one partition's replication, not a whole cluster, and that broker names and timings are not Kafka's.

**Phone.** Partition tapes use `CellGrid size="sm"` with at most 24 visible offsets (older ones scroll inside the row); the lag bars and ledger stack below. Scene 3's three rows fit at 390; the `SequenceDiagram` sits in its own `WideRegion`.

**`stream-windowing` · Stream Processing: Windows & Watermarks** **[domain review]**
Track `data` · *"Aggregating a river that never ends"* · Builds on: `message-queues`, `kafka-partitioned-log`
**Spec.**
- **Scene 1 · Event time vs processing time.** A seeded generator emits 40 click events: each has an **event time** (when it happened) and an **arrival time** = event time + a random delay in `[0, D]` (D set by a slider, 0 to 30 s, exponential-ish tail from `core/distributions` if K3-1 is available, else a seeded uniform). Events are drawn on two stacked timelines: event time (top) and arrival order (bottom) with lines between, so the crossing lines are the disorder. A **"count per minute"** panel shows two answers: by processing time (when it arrived) and by event time (when it happened). They disagree wherever a delay crosses a minute boundary.
- **Scene 2 · Windows.** Pick the window type: **tumbling** (size S), **sliding/hopping** (size S, slide L), **session** (gap G). Events are assigned live and coloured by window; a table lists each window's range and count.
  - Sliding windows with S a multiple of L put each event in S/L windows (shown as a badge); with L > S events can fall in no window (a "gap", allowed and labelled).
  - Session windows merge: two sessions separated by less than G become one when a bridging event arrives (animated merge).
  - A key picker (users A, B, C) makes sessions per key.
- **Scene 3 · Watermarks and lateness.** The watermark is `max event time seen - bound` (a "bounded out-of-orderness" strategy; stated as one common strategy, not the only one) and moves as events arrive. A window **fires** when the watermark passes its end. The learner sets `bound` (0 to 30 s) and **allowed lateness** (0 to 30 s):
  - an event for an already-fired window is **late**: if inside allowed lateness it **updates** the window (a "late firing" is drawn), otherwise it is **dropped** (or sent to a side output, a toggle) and the dropped count rises;
  - a **"send a straggler"** button injects an event 20 s behind the watermark.
  - The result panel shows streaming result vs **ground truth** (the batch answer over all events by event time), with the per-window difference in `--bad`, and the trade-off as three numbers: windows' result delay (watermark lag), dropped events, and state held (open windows).
- **Scene 4 · Processing-time windows (the broken way).** A toggle switches the aggregation to processing time, which looks fine at D = 0 and silently wrong at D > 0; a **replay** button re-runs the same events in reverse arrival order and shows the event-time result unchanged and the processing-time result different (replay safety).
**Out of scope.** Exactly-once, state stores and checkpointing, joins between streams, SQL streaming dialects, and partitioned parallelism (named; `kafka-partitioned-log` owns partitions). Watermarks per partition (the minimum across partitions) are one sentence and one tiny demo, not a scene.
**Kit.** `GanttLanes` (event vs arrival timelines and window spans; instants via K3-11 are not needed here, events are ticks via `markers`), `core/simTick` (K3-2: seeded fixed-tick generator, scrub for free), `LogStream` (K3-9: "window [60,120) fired: 7", "late event dropped"), `Plot2D` `Steps`/`Bars` for per-window counts vs truth (K3-1), `Stat` multi-line sub (K3-3), `Segmented`, `Slider`, `Toggle`, `ChipButton`, `Provenance`.
**AC.**
- **Watch:** on-time events `--ok`, late-but-accepted events `--info`, dropped events `--bad`; the watermark is `--hi`; open windows `--violet`, fired windows `--dim`; the ground-truth bar is `--ok` outline, the streamed bar tinted `--info`, a mismatch `--bad`.
- **Misconception:** "windows are closed by the clock" or "events arrive in order". The watermark closes them, and event time is the truth. A quiz question uses a scenario with a straggler.
- **Correctness (oracle: an offline batch group-by on the full event list):**
  - With `allowed lateness >= max delay`, streamed counts equal the offline counts for every window, for every seed (200 seeds, all three window types).
  - With a smaller bound, `truth - streamed` per window equals exactly the number of events dropped for that window (conservation, 200 seeds).
  - A sliding event belongs to `ceil(S/L)` or `floor(S/L)` windows exactly as the half-open definition `[start, start+S)` says (property on a grid of S, L); tumbling puts each event in exactly one window.
  - Session windows equal "sort the key's events and split where the gap exceeds G" (independent reference).
  - **Invariant for rule 20:** no event is counted twice in one tumbling window, and the watermark never moves backwards (checked at every tick). **Broken mode:** processing-time windows violate "window counts equal the event-time oracle" on the lowest seed with `D > 0` where a delay crosses a boundary; a test names that seed and the replay scene shows it.
- **Hostile input:** S, L, G, `bound` and lateness are bounded integers (S >= 1, G >= 1); L = 0 refused; negative delays impossible; an empty stream shows "no windows", not NaN.
- **Honesty:** chip "simulated, seed N", "bounded-out-of-orderness watermark is one strategy; Flink, Beam and Spark have their own APIs and defaults (from memory, verify)". The stage says watermarks are heuristics: a straggler beyond the bound is lost under the drop policy.

**Phone.** The two timelines scroll sideways in one `WideRegion` (`minWidth` from `minWidthForText`, the controls stay outside); the window table collapses to a stacked list.

**`count-min-heavy-hitters` · Count-Min Sketch & Heavy Hitters** **[domain review]**
Track `dsa` · *"Top-K without counting everything"* · Builds on: `probabilistic-structures`, `kafka-partitioned-log`
Recaps in one sentence what `probabilistic-structures` already teaches (Bloom: "no" is certain, HyperLogLog: distinct counts) and starts at **frequency** estimation, which that lesson does not cover. The builder reads its key ideas first (AC-0/T3 #25).
**Spec.**
- **Scene 1 · The grid.** A `d x w` counter grid (`CellGrid`, default d = 4, w = 16 so the cells are readable). The learner types an item or presses **Stream 30 items** (a seeded Zipf stream over 12 items). Each item hashes once per row; the d cells light in `--hi`, increment, and the **estimate** is the minimum over the d cells, with the winning cell ringed.
  - A **truth** column (a `Map`) sits beside the sketch; the error `estimate - true` is shown per item and is **never negative** (the stage says "only overcounts: collisions only add").
  - The hash family is a real pairwise-independent family `((a*x + b) mod p) mod w` with p = 2^31 - 1 and seeded `a, b` per row (x from `core/hash` FNV-1a of the item); shown as "row i uses its own a, b".
- **Scene 2 · Size vs error.** Sliders for width w (8 to 2048) and depth d (1 to 8), stream length N (up to 50,000) and Zipf skew s (0.6 to 1.6), over 2,000 distinct items. The guarantee panel states the theorem with the lesson's numbers: error at most `epsilon * N` with probability at least `1 - delta`, where `w = ceil(e / epsilon)` and `d = ceil(ln(1 / delta))` (Cormode and Muthukrishnan, 2005; verify against the paper). With the learner's `w`, `d` the panel computes epsilon and delta back (`epsilon = e / w`, `delta = e^-d`) and shows, from the actual run, the **fraction of items whose error exceeded epsilon * N** next to delta. A `Plot2D` shows error vs rank: heavy items have small *relative* error, rare items large.
  - Worked numbers on screen: `epsilon = 0.01, delta = 0.01` gives `w = 272, d = 5`, 1,360 counters.
- **Scene 3 · Heavy hitters.** Next to the sketch, a size-K min-heap of candidates (K = 5): for each arriving item, query the sketch and keep it if its estimate beats the heap minimum. The learner sees the heap track the true top 5 (a `Map` count shown for comparison), with precision and recall vs the exact top K, computed. A **"why a candidate heap?"** note: the sketch can answer "how many times did X occur" but cannot list its keys. Compare memory: exact counting stores one counter per distinct item (2,000) vs the sketch's `d * w`.
- **Scene 4 · Merge across partitions** (links `kafka-partitioned-log`). Three Kafka-partition streams each keep a sketch with the **same hash seeds**; adding the three grids cell by cell gives exactly the sketch of the combined stream (integer equality shown with a check mark computed from both). Using different seeds breaks it (the sum is wrong), a toggle that shows the corrupted estimates.
- **Scene 5 · Breaking it.** Toggles: **mean instead of min** (overcounts more; error plot jumps), **deletions (negative updates)** (the one-sided guarantee fails and the stage says why), and **tiny width** (w = 4: everything collides, estimates approach N).
**Out of scope.** Bloom filters and HyperLogLog (`probabilistic-structures`), exact top-K with a heap alone (named), conservative update and Count Sketch with signs (named in one sentence), sliding-window heavy hitters.
**Kit.** `CellGrid` (counters, `highlight` for the d cells), `Plot2D` (`Bars`, `Points`, `VLine` for epsilon*N), `core/stats` (`histogram`), K3-1 Zipf-capable sampler via `core/random` (`weightedPick`, or a Zipf helper local if K3-1 does not have one, filed as a Phase 2 request), `Stat` with K3-3 multi-line sub, `Segmented`, `Slider`, `Toggle`, `TextField`, `Provenance`, `createRecorder` for scene 1.
**AC.**
- **Watch:** the item's d cells are `--hi`; the minimum cell `--ok` ring; the collision-polluted cells `--bad` tint (cells holding more than one distinct item, computed); the truth column `--ok`; candidates in the heap `--info`; heap evictions `--dim`; the epsilon*N rule is a `--violet` `VLine`.
- **Misconception:** "a sketch can undercount like a Bloom filter has false positives" and "a bigger sketch makes it exact". It only overcounts, and it is exact only if w exceeds the distinct count *and* no two items collide, which is not guaranteed. A quiz question targets each.
- **Correctness:**
  - `estimate >= true` for every item across 200 seeded streams (with non-negative updates), and the estimate equals the minimum of the d touched cells (recomputed independently from the grid).
  - **Monte Carlo of the guarantee:** over 200 seeds, for fixed items, the fraction of runs with error above `epsilon * N` is at most delta plus a stated tolerance (0.02), for `(w, d) = (272, 5)` and `(55, 3)`.
  - Merge: `sketch(A) + sketch(B)` equals `sketch(A ++ B)` cell for cell, over 100 seeded splits, same seeds only; different seeds do not (broken variant must fail).
  - Heavy-hitter recall: with `w >= 272` and Zipf skew 1.2, the true top 5 are all in the heap on every one of 50 seeds (the test states this threshold; if it is not met, the lesson's default sizes change, not the test).
  - Pairwise hash family: each row's mapping is uniform within tolerance on 10,000 integers (chi-square style bound, tested, stated).
- **Hostile input:** w and d integers in bounds (w >= 1, d >= 1); an empty item is refused; N above 50,000 is refused; skew outside range is clamped with a message.
- **Honesty:** `Provenance`: "simulated, seed N", "toy hash family for teaching (real systems use stronger hashes such as murmur variants; from memory)". The theorem is credited and hedged; the stage says the bound is about the *additive error on total count*, which is meaningless for items rarer than epsilon * N.

**Phone.** The grid uses `CellGrid size="sm"` (d x 16 fits 390); scene 2's plot is `Plot2D` at default width; the heap stacks below.

**`inverted-index` · Search Engines & Inverted Indexes**
Track `data` · *"From words to documents in one lookup"* · Builds on: `hash-table`
**Spec.**
- **Scene 1 · Build the index.** A corpus of 12 short lesson-authored documents (about 8 to 14 words each) in an editable list: the learner edits a document's text, adds one (up to 30 documents), or deletes one. A **tokenizer pipeline** is toggled step by step: lowercase, split on non-alphanumerics, remove stop words (a 10-word list), apply a **toy stemmer** (strip a trailing `s`, `ed`, `ing`; labelled toy, not Porter). Each document's tokens flow into a **term to postings** table: `cat -> [d2, d5, d9]`, with term frequency per posting; the table is a `hash-table`-style map (a link back) and each posting list is kept **sorted by document id**.
- **Scene 2 · Query.** A query box (default `cat dog`) with **AND** and **OR** and a `NOT` token (`-word`). AND runs a **two-pointer merge** of the sorted posting lists, drawn as two cursors advancing (the same shape as `two-pointers`), with a comparison counter; OR merges, NOT subtracts. The lesson compares comparisons for "intersect the shorter list first" vs a naive order, computed.
  - A **phrase query** (`"cat sat"`) uses a positional index: postings carry positions and the merge checks position adjacency.
- **Scene 3 · Ranking.** Matches are ranked by **TF-IDF** or **BM25** with live sliders `k1` (0 to 3) and `b` (0 to 1). The formula is shown with the numbers substituted for the top result: `score(D, Q) = sum over q of IDF(q) * f * (k1 + 1) / (f + k1 * (1 - b + b * |D| / avgdl))`, with the IDF of the form `ln(1 + (N - n + 0.5) / (n + 0.5))` (the Lucene form; **verify against the Lucene/Robertson-Zaragoza BM25 description**). Defaults `k1 = 1.2`, `b = 0.75` are labelled "common defaults (from memory, verify)". The learner sees `b = 0` ignore length and `b = 1` fully normalise, and a long document with the term repeated 10 times saturate as `k1` falls.
- **Scene 4 · Compression and updates.** The postings of one frequent term are shown as **gaps** (delta-encoded ids) and then as **variable-byte** bytes (7 data bits per byte, high bit as the continuation flag; stated as one common scheme), with the byte count vs fixed 4-byte ids. A note and a button show **why updates are expensive**: adding one document appends to many posting lists (count shown), so real engines write immutable segments and merge them (named: `lsm-trees` is the same idea).
**Out of scope.** Sharding and distributed search, analyzers for other languages, fuzzy matching and spell correction (named), embedding-based retrieval (`vector-search`), learning to rank. Boolean operators beyond AND/OR/NOT, parentheses, and wildcard queries are not supported and the stage says so.
**Kit.** `TextField` (query), `TextArea` or a list of `TextField` for documents, `ChipButton` (pipeline steps, presets), `Segmented` (ranker), `Slider`, `ArrayStrip` (two posting lists with `pointers`), `CellGrid` or a table for the postings, `Tray`, `CodePanel` (the BM25 line with `renderLine` for the substituted numbers), `Stat` multi-line, `core/parse` for the query, `createRecorder` for the merge.
**AC.**
- **Watch:** the current posting being compared is `--hi`; ids present in both lists (the match) `--ok`; ids skipped `--dim`; the second cursor `--info`; matched query terms in the document text `--hi` highlights; the tokens removed by stop words `--dim`; terms the stemmer changed `--violet`.
- **Misconception:** "a search engine scans the documents for your words". It reads a precomputed term to documents map and merges lists; and "more occurrences always means a better match" (BM25 saturates). Two quiz questions.
- **Correctness:**
  - For 500 seeded queries over a seeded 40-document corpus, AND/OR/NOT results equal a brute-force scan of every document's token set (independent oracle).
  - Phrase results equal a brute-force scan for the token subsequence (no positional index in the oracle).
  - **BM25 hand vector** (k1 = 1.2, b = 0.75, the Lucene IDF, corpus `"the cat sat on the mat" / "the dog sat" / "cat and dog and cat"`, no stop words): `avgdl = 14/3`, `IDF(cat) = IDF(dog) = ln(1 + (3 - 2 + 0.5)/(2 + 0.5)) = 0.4700`; query `cat dog` gives scores 0.4208, 0.5504, 1.0902 for documents 1, 2, 3 (to four places; recomputed in the test by an independent formula, not by the lesson's function). Setting `b = 0`, `k1 = 0` gives each matching term exactly its IDF.
  - Gap and variable-byte encoding round-trips for 1,000 seeded sorted lists, including ids above 127 and 16,383 (the 1/2/3-byte boundaries).
  - The merge comparison count equals an instrumented reference.
- **Hostile input:** an empty query, a query of only stop words, and unknown terms give "no results" with the reason; a document over 280 characters is truncated with a notice; operator-only input (`-`, `""`) is a message, never an exception; the index is capped at 30 documents.
- **Honesty:** "toy corpus and toy stemmer; real engines use proper analyzers and quantised norms" and the default BM25 constants as from memory; the Lucene formula is named, not quoted as the only form.

**Phone.** The postings table is a list of rows (term, df, ids) that wraps; the two-list merge strip scrolls inside a `WideRegion`; sliders stack.

**`vector-search` · Vector Search & ANN Indexes** **[domain review]**
Track `data` · *"Nearest neighbours in a thousand dimensions"* · Builds on: `vectors-similarity`
Opens by quoting the Tier 2 fences: `vectors-similarity` ("ANN indexes are T3 `vector-search`") and `embeddings` ("narrowed to brute-force nearest neighbours; ANN indexes stay in T3 `vector-search`"), and starts exactly where brute force stops. Embedding models and RAG stay out (`embeddings`; T3 `rag-pipelines`, built later).
**Spec.**
- **Scene 1 · Exact search costs N.** 400 seeded 2-D points in 5 clusters (`blobs`). The learner drags the query point (`Plot2D` `Points onDrag` plus `PlotCursor` for keyboard); brute force computes N distances and returns the top k (k slider 1 to 10). The stat shows **distance computations = N**; a toggle switches cosine to Euclidean and shows both agree when vectors are unit length (`||a - b||^2 = 2 - 2cos`, asserted).
- **Scene 2 · IVF (cluster then probe).** k-means (from `clustering-kmeans`'s logic via `core/vec`; if a shared k-means is missing the builder files a Phase 2 request rather than copying) partitions the points into `nlist` cells (4 to 32, Voronoi shading with `Heat`). A query scans only the `nprobe` nearest cells (slider 1 to nlist). The stage shows candidates scanned, **recall@k** vs brute force and a **miss** case: the true neighbour lies across a cell border in an unprobed cell (`--bad` ring). Sweeping `nprobe` draws a real **recall vs candidates** curve, computed by running every `nprobe`.
- **Scene 3 · HNSW (a layered graph).** A real, simplified HNSW build on the same points (parameters `M` 2 to 8, `efConstruction`, `efSearch`; "simple neighbour selection, not the paper's heuristic", stated). Layers are drawn as stacked planes (`GraphView` fixed positions, layer picker or an exploded 3-plane view). **`segment-trees` · Segment Trees & Fenwick Trees**
Track `dsa` · *"Range queries with updates in log n"* · Builds on: `prefix-sums`
Opens by quoting `prefix-sums`' key ideas ("`sum(a[l..r]) = prefix[r + 1] - prefix[l]`: O(1) per query after an O(n) build" and "a difference array makes a range update two writes"); this lesson starts where they stop: queries mixed with point updates, and other operations than sum. This lesson **absorbs the Fenwick tree** (merge decision in the plan section 1.1); it stays within five scenes.
**Spec.**
- **Scene 1 · Why prefix sums stop.** An 8-cell array in `ArrayStrip` with the `prefix-sums` array under it. The learner changes one value: the prefix array rewrites every cell from there to the end (cells touched is counted: up to n). A query is O(1). The learner then runs a mixed workload (a seeded 20 operations, a ratio slider of updates to queries) and sees the totals for prefix sums vs the next two structures, in one unit ("array cells read or written").
- **Scene 2 · The segment tree.** The same array as a tree (`TreeSvg`, an index-ordered complete binary tree; node = range `[l, r]` and its aggregate). An operation picker: **sum, min, max, gcd**. **Query [l, r]:** the minimal covering nodes light up (`--ok`), nodes visited but only partly covered `--info`, and the answer is the combination of the covering nodes; at most `2 * ceil(log2 n)` covering nodes. **Point update:** the leaf changes and every ancestor recomputes along the path to the root (animated upward, `ceil(log2 n) + 1` nodes). Drag handles or typed `l`, `r` choose the range.
- **Scene 3 · Range update with lazy propagation.** "Add v to all of [l, r]": fully covered nodes get a **lazy tag** (drawn as a small badge) instead of updating their children; a later query or update that must descend **pushes the tag down** one level (animated). **`training-vs-inference` · Training vs Inference Serving** **[domain review]**
Track `ml` · *"Learning is batch; predicting is latency"* · Builds on: (none; links `queueing-theory` and `percentiles-statistics` from wave 1 for the latency maths)
Boundary: the Tier 4 `llm-serving` lesson (KV cache, prefill/decode, continuous batching) is built on this one; this lesson stays at the whole-system level and does not teach it (named in words, no link).
**Spec.**
- **Scene 1 · Two loops.** A split screen. **Left, training:** a loop of `forward -> loss -> backward -> update` over minibatches of a real tiny model (a `core/nn` logistic or small MLP on seeded blobs, trained in the browser) with a falling loss curve and epochs counter; throughput in samples per second is shown as a **model-based number** (the number of multiply-adds counted from the shapes, divided by a labelled illustrative device rate), not a wall-clock reading. **Right, inference:** requests with a deadline, a single forward pass each, latency shown per request. The point on screen: training optimises **total work over a dataset** and tolerates stalls; serving optimises **time per request** and must not.
- **Scene 2 · Memory: why training needs more.** A calculator with a **parameters** slider (1 million to 10 billion; illustrative sizes labelled as such) and a **regime** picker. Inference weights in half precision cost 2 bytes per parameter; **training with Adam in mixed precision** is commonly accounted as 16 bytes per parameter (2 for half-precision weights, 2 for gradients, 12 for the full-precision master weights and the two Adam moments: Rajbhandari et al., ZeRO, 2020; **verify**), before activations, which grow with batch size and sequence length. The panel shows bytes per parameter by component (a stacked `Bar` with segments), the total for the chosen size and the ratio (computed: 8x for the default), against a labelled illustrative device memory. A **gradient accumulation / smaller batch** note explains the activation lever. - **Scene 3 · Serving: dynamic batching.** A seeded Poisson stream of requests at rate `lambda` (10 to 400 per second) feeds one server whose batch service time is `s(b) = a + c b` with illustrative constants `a = 20 ms` and `c = 2 ms` (a fixed per-batch cost that batching amortises). The learner sets **max batch size B** (1 to 32) and **max wait T** (0 to 50 ms): a batch goes when B requests are queued or T has elapsed since the first. - **Scene 5 · Checkpoints.** A training job of 10,000 steps (one second each, illustrative) on hardware that fails at random with a mean time between failures `M` (slider); a **checkpoint interval K** (steps) with a per-checkpoint cost. A seeded Monte Carlo (100 runs per K) plots expected total time against K: a U-shape, with the optimum near the classical `sqrt(2 * cost * M)` estimate (**verify: Young/Daly**; shown as a marker, not as the truth, with the simulated minimum next to it).
**Out of scope.** Distributed training strategies (data/tensor/pipeline parallelism, named), GPU architecture (`simd-gpu-parallelism`, a later wave), KV cache and continuous batching (Tier 4 `llm-serving`), model compression and quantisation (named), feature stores, MLOps tooling.

**`columnar-storage` · Columnar Storage**
Track `data` · *"Store by column, scan only what you need"* · Builds on: (none)
**Spec.**
- **Scene 1 · Same table, two layouts.** A 16-row table (`id`, `country`, `product`, `qty`, `price`; seeded, lesson-authored values, every field a fixed 4 bytes in this lesson to keep the arithmetic exact, said so) laid out in **pages** of 64 bytes (`CellGrid` with K3-16 `rowDecor`: a rail per page). The **row store** packs whole rows, 3 rows per page (rows never straddle a page in this model; stated), so 16 rows take 6 pages. The **column store** packs each column separately: one page per column for 16 values (16 x 4 B = 64 B). The learner picks a query (`SELECT SUM(price)`, `SELECT AVG(qty) WHERE country = 'NL'`, `SELECT * WHERE id = 7`, `INSERT one row`, `UPDATE one price`) and the stage **lights the pages each layout must read or write**, with a counter in **bytes touched = pages x 64** for both (one function, one unit). Defaults give the headline: `SUM(price)` touches 6 pages = 384 B in the row store and 1 page = 64 B in the column store (6x).
- **Scene 2 · Where rows win.** `SELECT * WHERE id = 7` reads 1 page in the row store and 5 pages (one per column) in the column store; `INSERT` writes 1 page vs 5; the table of all five queries and both layouts shows **neither layout wins everywhere** (a table computed from the same two functions). The stage ties this to OLTP vs OLAP in words (the next lesson owns the system view).
- **Scene 3 · Encodings.** Per-column controls: **none**, **run-length** (value, count pairs), **dictionary** (distinct values get codes of `ceil(log2 distinct)` bits, bit-packed), **delta** for sorted integers, with a **sort-by** picker (unsorted, by country, by price). The learner sees `country` sorted collapse to a few runs (RLE) while the same column unsorted barely shrinks, and the bytes (bits, rounded up to whole bytes, rule stated) per column before and after. Encodings **round-trip live**: a "decode" panel rebuilds the column from the encoded form and shows equality with a check computed from both.
- **Scene 4 · Skip what you can.** The 16 rows are grouped into 4 **row groups** of 4; each group keeps **min and max per column** (zone maps). A filter `WHERE qty > 8` (slider threshold) greys the row groups whose max is at or below the threshold and counts the groups and bytes **skipped**. Sorting changes how many groups can be skipped (computed). Names `row group`, `column chunk` and `page` are the vocabulary of Parquet-style files (**from memory; verify against the Parquet documentation**), not a claim that this model reproduces the format.
- **Scene 5 · Vectorised scans.** A counter compares **function calls** to sum a column of N values: one interpreted call per row vs one call per 1,024-value batch (`ceil(N / 1024)` calls), as a computed illustration of why batch-at-a-time engines amortise per-row overhead. **No timings are shown** and the stage says wall-clock speed depends on the engine and the CPU.
**Out of scope.** The warehouse/lake system view and table formats (`warehouse-lake-lakehouse`), query optimisation (`query-planning`, built earlier, linked only for "what the planner chooses"), compression codecs such as LZ4 and Zstandard (named; `lz-compression` is a later wave), updates and deletes in column stores beyond a note, and bit-level SIMD (`simd-gpu-parallelism`).
**Kit.** `CellGrid` (pages, rows, with K3-16 `rowDecor` rails), `core/grid`, `ByteGrid` (the encoded bytes of one column), `Plot2D` (`Bars` for bytes touched), `core/format` (bytes), `Segmented` (query, encoding, sort), `Slider` (threshold), `ChipButton`, `Stat` multi-line sub, `createRecorder`, `useScenePicker`, `Provenance`.
**AC.**
- **Watch:** the pages a query must touch are `--hi` with a byte count; pages not touched `--dim`; the cheap layout's total `--ok` and the costly layout's `--bad` *only within a given query* (the colours swap on the insert/point query, so they follow the computed comparison, never a fixed "columns good"); a skipped row group `--ok` tick with `--dim` fill; encoded runs are categorical tones, never `--ok`/`--bad`.

- **Correctness (oracles: a naive reduce, a hand-worked case, round-trips):**
  - **Hand-worked case:** 16 rows x 5 fixed 4-byte fields, 64-byte pages: row store `floor(64 / 20) = 3` rows per page, `ceil(16 / 3) = 6` pages = 384 B for `SUM(price)`; column store 64 B; ratio 6.

**`mapreduce-batch` · Batch Processing & MapReduce**
Track `data` · *"Split, shuffle, reduce"* · Builds on: (none)
**Spec.**
- **Scene 1 · Word count, by hand.** The input text (preset or typed, up to 200 words) is split into **M splits** (slider 1 to 6); each **map task** emits `(word, 1)` pairs (the pairs are drawn leaving each mapper); the **shuffle** partitions pairs by `hash(word) mod R` (R reducers, slider 1 to 4; `core/hash`) and sorts within a partition; each **reduce task** sums per word. The stage shows the final table next to a plain single-process count (the independent reference) with an equality tick. Example: `the cat and the dog and the bird` gives `the 3, and 2, cat 1, dog 1, bird 1`.
- **Scene 2 · The combiner.** A toggle runs a **combiner** (a local reduce on each mapper's output before the shuffle). The stage counts **records crossing the shuffle** with and without it (a computed number in one unit: key-value records), and states the condition: a combiner is only valid when the reduce is **associative and commutative** (sum yes; average no unless carrying (sum, count)). A second job ("average length of words per first letter") shows the wrong answer when the learner applies a naive averaging combiner, with the correct (sum, count) version beside it.
- **Scene 3 · Skew and stragglers.** A **skew preset** (one word is 60% of the input) sends most records to one reducer; a Gantt (`GanttLanes`, workers as lanes, tasks as bars, progressive via K3-10 `barAt`) shows one reducer running long while the others idle. A **speculative backup** toggle starts a duplicate of the slow task on an idle worker and takes whichever finishes first; with a deterministic task, the output is the same, and the job's wall time (in simulated ticks) is shown to fall only if the slowness is the machine's, not the data's (a data-skewed reducer is slow on the backup too: the lesson shows both cases, computed).
- **Scene 4 · A worker dies.** The learner kills a worker mid-run (button or click a bar). The scheduler **re-executes its tasks**. The stage explains the rule from the original system description: map output sits on the mapper's local disk, so even **completed map tasks on a dead worker are redone**, while completed reduce output is already in the shared file system and is not (**from memory of the original MapReduce paper by Dean and Ghemawat, 2004; verify**). The result table stays equal to the reference after any kill schedule (shown with the equality tick).
- **Scene 5 · When tasks are not safe to re-run.** A toggle makes the map function **non-idempotent**: it also increments a counter in an external store for every record it processes. After a kill and re-execution the counter exceeds the input size, while the word counts stay right (the side effect leaked, drawn as "counter 41 > 33 records"); the stage names the rule "tasks must be deterministic and side-effect free, or idempotent, for retries to be safe" and links to `idempotency-keys`.
**Out of scope.** Spark and in-memory dataflow (named), HDFS block placement, YARN scheduling, joins and secondary sort (one note, a reduce-side join named), streaming (`stream-windowing`, wave 4).
**Kit.** `GanttLanes` (workers and tasks; `kind: 'wait'` for waiting reducers; K3-10 `barAt` for progressive bars), `core/simTick` (K3-2: seeded durations and kill schedule, scrubbable), `core/hash` (`fnv1a` for partitioning), `Tray` (pairs), a table (`CellGrid` or HTML) for the result, `Segmented`, `Slider`, `Toggle`, `TextField`/`TextArea`, `ChipButton`, `Stat`, `LogStream` (K3-9) for scheduler events, `Provenance`.
**AC.**
- **Watch:** map tasks `--info`, reduce tasks `--violet`, a waiting reducer hatched `--violet`; the straggler `--hi`; a killed worker and re-executed tasks `--bad`; the finished correct table `--ok`; records saved by the combiner `--ok`, records still shuffled `--info`; the leaked counter `--bad`.
- **Misconception:** "MapReduce is slow because it is old" (the point is the programming model: independent tasks that can be retried) and "a worker failure loses the job" (tasks are re-run). Two quiz questions; a third on why the combiner cannot average naively.
- **Correctness (oracles: a single-process word count; invariants over seeded schedules, AC-0/T3 #20):**
  - **Invariant (safety):** for every `(M, R)` in 1..6 x 1..4, with or without the combiner and speculation, and for 200 seeded kill schedules (up to 2 failures, arbitrary timing), the final word counts **equal the single-process count exactly**, and **no record is counted twice** (each input record is reduced exactly once in the final output).
  - **Broken mode:** a non-idempotent map (scene 5) violates the invariant "side-effect counter equals input size" on the lowest seed where a kill hits a worker holding completed map tasks; the test names that seed and the scene shows it. A second broken variant, "the combiner averages averages", gives wrong averages on the learner-visible preset (a test with numbers).
  - Shuffle partitioning sends every key to exactly one reducer and the same key always to the same reducer (property over 10,000 words); the combiner never increases the records shuffled (asserted over 200 seeded inputs).
  - The Gantt is consistent with the schedule: no worker runs two tasks at once; a re-executed task starts after the failure time (checked per run).
  - **Liveness** is stated separately: with all workers dead the job stalls ("no worker left: the job cannot finish"), never reported as done.
- **Hostile input:** empty input produces an empty result with "no words"; punctuation and case normalised by a stated rule (lowercase, split on non-letters); words longer than 24 characters truncated for display only; M, R bounded; killing a worker twice or when none is left is a no-op with a message.
- **Honesty:** `Provenance`: "simulated cluster, seed N: durations are seeded numbers, not measurements"; the dead-worker rule is "from memory of the 2004 paper, verify"; the stage says real clusters have many more tasks than workers and the toy has a handful.

**`kmp` · KMP String Matching**
Track `dsa` · *"Never re-read what you already matched"* · Builds on: `rolling-hash`
**Spec.**
- **Scene 1 · The naive slide.** Text and pattern in two `ArrayStrip` rows (the learner types both; text up to 40 characters, pattern up to 12; presets below). The naive algorithm aligns the pattern at each shift, compares left to right and, on a mismatch, **slides by one and starts over**; the stepper shows each comparison with a counter, and the characters it **already matched and is about to read again** are tinted `--bad` ("rework"). The preset `aaaab` in `aaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaa` (20 `a`s) costs **80** comparisons.
- **Scene 2 · The failure table, built live.** For each prefix of the pattern, `lps[i]` is the length of the longest **proper** prefix that is also a suffix of `pattern[0..i]`. It is built beside the pattern by matching **the pattern against itself** (a second pointer sliding over its own prefix; the same fall-back rule as the matcher), step by step. Examples shown as presets: `abcabd` -> `0 0 0 1 2 0`; `aaaab` -> `0 1 2 3 0`; `abababac` -> `0 0 1 2 3 4 5 0`; `aabaaab` -> `0 1 0 1 2 2 3`. A hover on `lps[i]` highlights the prefix and the suffix it equates.
- **Scene 3 · The match.** KMP keeps a **text pointer that never moves back** and a pattern pointer `j`. On a match both advance; on a mismatch with `j > 0` it sets `j = lps[j - 1]` (the pattern shifts right *without re-reading the text*), and with `j = 0` the text pointer advances. After a full match it records the position and continues with `j = lps[m - 1]`, so **overlapping matches are found** (`aa` in `aaaa` -> positions 0, 1, 2). The stepper and a compact `CodePanel` run together; each fall-back shows an arrow from `j` to `lps[j - 1]` on the pattern strip with "shift by `j - lps[j-1]`".
- **Scene 4 · Count the work.** Both algorithms run on the same input and the stage compares **character comparisons**, one unit for both, counting every compare including the ones that cause a fall-back. A bar shows naive 80 vs KMP 36 on the `aaaab` preset (`<= 2n = 40`); a `Plot2D` sweeps text length n for a worst-case pair and a random pair, from real runs, with the line `2n`. The amortised argument is shown as two counters: the text pointer only increases (at most n) and every fall-back decreases `j`, which can only have been increased by an advance (so at most n fall-backs).
**Out of scope.** Rabin-Karp and rolling hashes (`rolling-hash`), Boyer-Moore and Z-algorithm (named), Aho-Corasick (named only), regular expressions (`regex-engines`, a later wave: not linked), and Unicode normalisation.
**Kit.** `ArrayStrip` (text and pattern rows with `pointers`, `ranges`, `cellState`), `CodePanel` (the loop, `stateOf`), `TextField` (text and pattern, `sanitize` to printable characters), `ChipButton` presets, `createRecorder`, `Stat`, `Bar`, `Plot2D` (`Curve`, `HLine`), `core/parse` for lengths, `WideRegion` for long text, `Provenance` not required (nothing simulated).
**AC.**
- **Watch:** the comparison in progress `--hi`; matched characters `--ok`; the mismatch `--bad`; characters already read that naive re-reads `--bad` tint (rework) and KMP's skipped re-reads `--ok`; the text pointer `--info`, the pattern pointer `--violet`; the `lps` fall-back arrow `--violet`; ruled-out shifts `--dim`.
- **Misconception:** "KMP is faster because it compares fewer characters per alignment" (it never aligns again: the text pointer never moves back), and "the failure table depends on the text" (it depends only on the pattern). Two quiz questions.
- **Correctness (oracles: a brute-force scan, `lps` from the definition, counts):**
  - **Exhaustive `lps`:** for **every** binary string of length 1 to 12 (8,190 strings), `lps` equals the definition computed by trying every border length (independent code); plus the four hand vectors above.
  - Matches equal a naive all-occurrence scan for 500 seeded (text, pattern) pairs over a 2-letter alphabet (to force many partial matches), including overlapping matches, pattern equal to text, pattern longer than text (no matches, 0 comparisons) and a pattern of one character.
  - **Work bound:** KMP's comparisons are at most `2n` on every tested input (the 500 pairs plus the adversarial family `a^(n-1) b` against `a^k b`), and the naive count on the `aaaab` preset is exactly 80 (16 windows x 5), KMP's exactly 36.
  - **Broken variants:** a matcher that on mismatch sets `j = lps[j]` (off by one) or `j = 0` misses or misreports matches on a named seed; the test asserts the oracle rejects it.
- **Hostile input:** an **empty pattern is refused** with "a pattern needs at least one character (a scan for the empty string matches everywhere, so there is nothing to show)"; the text is capped at 40 and the pattern at 12 for the drawing, with the model unrestricted in tests; characters are UTF-16 code units (an emoji counts as two; stated on screen if one is typed).
- **Honesty:** "Nothing simulated: every comparison is counted from the run you see." The `2n` bound is derived on screen from the two counters, and the lesson says it counts character comparisons, not CPU time (a real `indexOf` may use other algorithms).

**Phone.** The two strips share a `WideRegion` that follows the active window (`reveal`); the `lps` table sits above it; the code panel collapses below.

**`convex-hull` · Convex Hull & Orientation Tests**
Track `dsa` · *"Wrap a rubber band around the points"* · Builds on: (none)
**Spec.**
- **Scene 1 · The turn test.** Three draggable points `a`, `b`, `c` on a math-axes plane (y up; the convention is on screen). The **cross product** `(b - a) x (c - a) = (bx - ax)(cy - ay) - (by - ay)(cx - ax)` is shown with the numbers substituted and a verdict: **positive = left turn (counter-clockwise), negative = right turn (clockwise), zero = collinear**. **`ropes-gap-buffers` · Ropes & Gap Buffers**
Track `dsa` · *"How text editors stay fast"* · Builds on: `arrays-linked-lists`
Starts from `arrays-linked-lists`' key idea ("array insert near the front is O(n): everything after it has to shift") and asks what an editor that inserts at the cursor all day should do instead. (The backlog also lists `crdts`; the plan re-points this to `arrays-linked-lists` and collaborative-editing structures are named in words only.)
**Spec.**
- **Convention (on screen once):** positions and the cursor count **Unicode code points**; a user-perceived character (a grapheme, such as an emoji with a skin-tone modifier) can be several code points and is not handled here.
- **Scene 1 · The plain array.** A text of up to 60 characters drawn as cells (`ArrayStrip`). The learner types, deletes and moves the cursor (click, arrow keys, Home/End). An insert at position `i` **shifts every character after it**: the shifted cells animate and a counter adds `n - i` **moves**. Inserting at the front of a long text vs at the end shows the cost gap (presets: "type at the end", "type at the start").
- **Scene 2 · The gap buffer.** The text is split by a **gap** of empty cells at the cursor (`before | gap | after`, the gap drawn hatched). **Insert** writes into the gap and shrinks it (O(1)); **moving the cursor by d** moves d characters across the gap (the gap travels, a counter adds `d` moves); when the gap is full, the buffer **doubles capacity** and copies (shown, counted, amortised). The learner types a sentence (cheap), then **jumps** to the far end and types one letter (the gap crosses the whole text: expensive once), then types more (cheap again). A "typical editing" preset (many inserts, short cursor moves) vs "search and replace across the file" (many far jumps) makes the trade-off concrete.
- **Scene 3 · The rope.** The same text as a **balanced binary tree**: leaves hold short pieces (up to 8 characters) and each internal node stores the **weight** (length of its left subtree). **Index `i`**: walk down using weights (go left if `i < weight`, else subtract and go right). **Concatenate** two ropes: a new root, `O(1)`. **Split at `i`**: walk down and cut, creating `O(log n)` new nodes. **Insert** = split, then concatenate three pieces; **delete a range** = two splits and one concat. The tree is drawn (`TreeSvg`), with **shared (unchanged) subtrees kept in place** and the new nodes highlighted. A **rebalance** step rebuilds the rope from its leaves when the depth exceeds `2 x ceil(log2 leaves)` (a simplified rule, stated; Boehm, Atkinson and Plass's "Ropes: an Alternative to Strings" (1995) describes a Fibonacci-length scheme; **verify**).
- **Scene 4 · The workload picker.** The learner picks a workload (type at the cursor; jump around and insert; paste a large block at the start; delete a large range; undo the last 10 edits) and a text size (1,000 to 1,000,000 characters, model-only above the drawn size) and the stage runs the same seeded operations on the **array, gap buffer and rope** and plots **touches** per operation, where **one touch is one character copied or one tree node read or created** (the unit is defined once and applied to all three rows; the stage says it does not equal wall time because a character copy is far cheaper than a node visit on real hardware). The result table shows each structure winning somewhere. A note names the **piece table** (an editor structure that keeps original text and an append-only buffer, with edits as a list of pieces) and states which editors use which structure **as common knowledge to verify**: Emacs's gap buffer, and a piece-tree design in some modern editors (**verify against the editors' documentation**).
**Out of scope.** CRDT and OT sequences for collaborative editing (named; `crdts` is a registered Tier 4 lesson, not linked here), piece tables beyond one panel, text rendering, line indexes and syntax trees, and grapheme clustering.
**Kit.** `ArrayStrip` (array and gap buffer rows; `cellState` for the gap and shifted cells, `pointers` for the cursor), `TreeSvg` (rope with `renderNode` for weights and leaf text, `nodeMark` for new/shared), `createRecorder`, `TextField` (type and edit), `ChipButton`, `Segmented`, `Slider`, `Toggle`, `Plot2D` (`Bars` for touches, log y), `core/scale`, `core/random`, `Stat` multi-line sub, `Tray`.
**AC.**
- **Watch:** the cursor `--info`; characters moved by an insert `--bad` (the costly side) with the count; the gap `--dim` hatched; the characters written `--hi`; the rope's new nodes `--hi`, unchanged shared subtrees `--violet`; the winning structure on a workload `--ok` and the loser `--bad` *for that workload only* (computed from the touches, the colours swap between workloads); weights drawn as numbers on nodes.
- **Misconception:** "a rope is just a linked list of strings" (it is a tree with weights, so indexing is `O(log n)`), and "gap buffers are always better than arrays" (a far jump is a full copy). Two quiz questions.
- **Correctness (oracle: a plain string splice, run after every operation):**
  - For 300 seeds x 200 operations (insert, delete a range, move the cursor, paste a block), the **array, gap buffer and rope all equal the reference string** after every operation, with code points including multi-byte characters.
  - **Rope invariants after every operation:** each internal node's weight equals the length of its left subtree; the leaves' concatenation equals the content; `index(i)` equals `string[i]` for every `i`; `split(i)` then `concat` returns the original; leaf size is within its limit except for the allowed short final leaf; depth after `rebalance` is at most the stated bound.
  - **Gap buffer invariants:** `before + after` equals the content, the cursor equals `len(before)`, capacity is never below content length, and a **cursor move of `d` costs exactly `d` moves** and an insert in a non-empty gap exactly 1 (asserted against the counters).
  - Touch counts: an array insert at `i` costs exactly `n - i` moves (hand-worked, e.g. 60 characters at position 10 costs 50); the rope's split visits at most `depth` nodes.
  - **Broken variants:** a rope whose weight is not updated on split, and a gap buffer that forgets to move the gap before an insert at a new cursor, are rejected by the string-equality oracle on a named seed.
- **Hostile input:** typing is limited to printable characters; a pasted block is capped at 200 characters for the drawn scene (the model workload scene has no cap); the cursor is clamped to `0..n`; delete on an empty text is a no-op with a message; lone surrogates are refused with a note.
