**`routing-algorithms` · Routing: Distance Vector vs Link State**
Track `net` · *"How packets find their way"* · Builds on: `graph-search`; soft link to `shortest-paths-advanced` (Bellman-Ford, wave 3) · **[domain review]**
Tier 2 fence quoted: `ethernet-arp` stops at "VLANs, STP, Wi-Fi (moved to T3) and IPv6 NDP"; `subnetting-cidr` owns prefixes. This lesson starts at "which next hop".
**Spec.**
- **Scene 1, forwarding vs routing.** Six routers A-F, weighted links (cost 1-9). Each router has a table (destination, next hop, cost). "Send a packet A to F" walks it hop by hop, each router consulting only its own table. One row shows two overlapping prefixes with longest-prefix match (the table is a teaching table, labelled). The caption separates the *control plane* (how tables get built) from the *data plane* (using them).
- **Scene 2, distance vector (the Bellman-Ford idea).**
  - Synchronous rounds. Each router sends its vector (destination to cost) to its neighbours; the new cost to D is the minimum over neighbours of `link cost + neighbour's advertised cost`. Cells that changed in a round light up.
  - Click a link to cut it or to set its cost (1-9); the table re-converges round by round. A "rounds to converge" counter reads from the model.
  - Infinity is 16 (RIP's metric limit, 15 hops usable; verify against RFC 2453). A toggle picks **plain**, **split horizon** (do not advertise a route back to the neighbour it came from) or **poisoned reverse** (advertise it back as 16).
  - Preset A: chain A-B-C, cut B-C. Plain DV counts up (see Correctness for the exact numbers); split horizon and poisoned reverse converge in two rounds.
  - Preset B: the triangle (A, B, C fully connected, X hanging off A, cut A-X). Split horizon and poisoned reverse **still count upward** here, because the loop spans three routers. The builder finds the exact preset by searching topologies of 5 routers or fewer, and a test pins the round counts. The caption says only what the model shows.
- **Scene 3, link state (the Dijkstra idea).**
  - On `K3-8 distSim`: each router floods a link-state advertisement (LSA: origin, sequence number, its links) to all neighbours; a router stores an LSA only if its sequence number is higher than the stored one, then forwards it to every neighbour except the one it came from.
  - After flooding settles, every router runs Dijkstra on its own copy of the link-state database and the shortest-path tree for a chosen router is drawn in `--ok`. A message counter and a "time until all databases agree" readout compare with scene 2's rounds.
  - Controls: cut or restore a link (both endpoint routers flood a new LSA), a latency range (min, max ms), a loss slider. Seed shown.
  - Mode **broken: ignore sequence numbers** (always overwrite). With reordered delivery an old LSA overwrites a newer one and a router keeps a route over a link that is down.
- Preset chips: "chain", "triangle + stub", "ring of 6", "two paths of equal cost" (shows equal-cost multipath: both next hops listed, named only).
**Out of scope.** OSPF areas, designated routers, LSA ageing, IS-IS details, RIP timers (30 s update, 180 s timeout: one chip, verify against RFC 2453), BGP (next lesson), MPLS, ECMP hashing.
**Kit.** `GraphView` (fixed layout, `edgeMark`, `onEdgeClick`, `nodeMark`, K3-5 underlay unused here), `.data-table` with K3-4 sortable header for the routing table, `useScenePicker`, `Segmented` (plain, split horizon, poisoned reverse), `Toggle`, `Slider`, `ChipButton` presets, `SequenceDiagram` via `toSequence(trace)` for the LSA flood, `createCluster` and `forSeeds`, `createRecorder`, `core/ipv4` (`parseCidr`, `contains`).
**AC.**
- **Watch:** a link in use by the selected route `--hi`, a cut link `--bad` dashed, a changed table cell `--hi` for the round it changed, an unreachable entry shown as "∞ (16)" in `--bad`, the Dijkstra tree `--ok`, an LSA in flight `--info`, a dropped LSA `--bad`, a stale LSA copy `--orange`.
- **Misconception:** "routers know the whole path to the destination" (distance vector routers know only the next hop and a cost), and "link-state converges instantly" (flooding takes time and a router can briefly hold a stale database). A quiz question targets each.
- **Correctness:**
  - The converged DV table equals Floyd-Warshall and Dijkstra on 200 seeded random connected graphs (6-10 nodes, costs 1-9); rounds to converge are at most n-1 (the Bellman-Ford bound).
  - Count to infinity is pinned to hand-worked values: chain A-B-C, cut B-C, plain DV, synchronous rounds with the advertised vectors taken at the start of the round. A's cost to C after rounds 1-6 is 2, 4, 4, 6, 6, 8 and B's is 3, 3, 5, 5, 7, 7, reaching 16 and stopping there.
  - Split horizon converges that case in 2 rounds (A learns "unreachable" from B's silence). Preset B's round counts are asserted for all three modes; the plain mode must be the slowest.
  - **Invariant (AC-0/T3 #20), K3-8:** over 200 seeds with delay, loss and duplication, with sequence numbers honoured, a router's stored sequence number for each origin never decreases at any step; after the flood settles and the lossless links stay up, all routers in a connected component hold identical databases and their next hops match Dijkstra on the true graph. Loss is retried by periodic re-flood (stated, since a lost LSA is otherwise never repaired).
  - **Broken mode violates it:** "ignore sequence numbers" breaks monotonicity on the first seed where a delayed old LSA is delivered after the newer one. A scripted schedule (old LSA latency 40 ms, new LSA 10 ms, same link) reproduces it on every seed, and the test also records the lowest violating seed in `forSeeds(0..199)` as a constant and shows it with its trace. A test fails if none exists.
- **Hostile input:** costs are integers 1-9 (`parseBoundedInt`); cutting every link leaves isolated routers marked "unreachable", never NaN or Infinity; a cost field containing text, 0 or 1e9 is refused with its reason.
- **Honesty:** `Provenance` simulated (seed), `from-memory` on RIP's 16 and update timers; the stage says "synchronous rounds are a teaching simplification; real routers send updates on timers" and "real OSPF has areas and ageing; this is the core idea".
**Phone.** `GraphView` with six nodes fits 390 (natural width about 340). The routing table and counters stack below. The LSA `SequenceDiagram` sits in its own `WideRegion` (about 480).

**`bgp` · BGP & Internet Routing**
Track `net` · *"The internet's handshake agreements"* · Builds on: `routing-algorithms`, `cdn-edge` · **[domain review]**
Fence quoted: `cdn-edge` names anycast without the routing behind it; `routing-algorithms` ends at one network run by one operator.
**Spec.**
- **Topology.** Eight fictional autonomous systems (AS64496-AS64503) drawn as `GraphView` nodes inside tinted regions (K3-5 underlay: "tier-1 core", "regional ISPs", "edge networks"). Edges are **customer-to-provider** (solid, arrow toward the provider) or **peer-to-peer** (dashed). The origin AS64500 owns `203.0.113.0/24` (documentation prefix).
- **Scene 1, announcements.** Step the propagation. An UPDATE carries the prefix and an AS_PATH; each AS prepends its own number when it passes the route to a neighbour. An AS that sees its own number in a received path discards it (loop prevention). A path table per AS lists candidate paths (K3-4 sortable).
- **Scene 2, choosing among paths.** A simplified decision process, labelled as such: (1) highest local preference, set by business relationship (routes from customers over peers over providers), (2) shortest AS_PATH, (3) lowest neighbour AS number as a stand-in tie-break (the real process continues with origin type, MED, eBGP over iBGP, IGP cost and router ID; verify against RFC 4271 §9.1.2). The learner edits one AS's local-preference per neighbour and watches the chosen next hop and everyone downstream change.
- **Scene 3, who may tell whom (export policy).** Toggle **valley-free (customer routes to everyone; peer and provider routes only to customers)** vs **leak everything**. In leak mode, a small multihomed customer AS re-announces one provider's route to its other provider, which now prefers it (customer route), so a transit flow lands on a thin link; a capacity bar on that link overflows. The caption says "a route leak" and does not name real incidents (verify before any is named).
- **Scene 4, hijack and anycast.** A second AS announces the same prefix. The learner picks the announcing AS and the length (/24 or /25). Equal length: each AS goes to whichever origin has the shorter path (a split map, which is also exactly how anycast works when the second origin is legitimate; one chip says so). A /25 inside the /24: longest-prefix match wins everywhere the /25 propagates, regardless of path length. A slider sets the share of ASes doing **route origin validation** (RPKI ROAs with a max length; a /25 against `maxLength 24` is invalid and dropped) and the reach map shows which ASes still reach the real origin. Path-forgery (an AS prepending the true origin to its own path) is shown as "not caught by origin validation"; BGPsec and ASPA are named only.
**Out of scope.** iBGP, route reflectors, communities and MED (named), BGP session mechanics (TCP port 179, keepalives, shown not run), real prefixes or ASNs, traffic engineering, blackholing (`ddos-mitigation` may name it later).
**Kit.** `GraphView` with `underlay` and `edgeMark` (K3-5), K3-4 sortable table, `Segmented`, `Toggle`, `Slider` (validation share), `ScenePicker`, `Bar` (link capacity), `core/ipv4` (`parseCidr`, `contains`, `networkOf`) plus the longest-prefix helper above, `createRecorder`.
**AC.**
- **Watch:** the origin AS `--ok`, an AS routing to the real origin `--ok`-ringed, an AS routing to the other announcer `--bad`, a path being announced `--info`, the chosen path `--hi`, a discarded loop `--dim`, customer-provider edges solid `--violet`, peer edges dashed `--violet`. Regions are plain categorical tints (`sky`, `amber`, `pink`), never `--ok`/`--bad`.
- **Misconception:** "BGP picks the fastest path" (it applies policy first, then shortest AS path; there is no latency in the decision), and "the shortest path always wins a hijack" (a more specific prefix beats it). A quiz question targets each.
- **Correctness:**
  - On 200 seeded random AS graphs generated valley-free (a customer-provider DAG plus peer edges), the converged choice at every AS equals an independent reference: enumerate all valley-free simple paths by DFS and take the best by (preference, length, tie-break). Every selected path is valley-free and loop-free. (Convergence under these conditions is the Gao-Rexford result; verify against Gao and Rexford, 2001.)
  - Forwarding at each AS picks the longest matching prefix; checked against a naive scan of all announcements on seeded prefix sets. With validation share 0 a /25 hijack reaches every AS that hears it; with 100% no AS selects it.
  - Origin validation is checked with the ROA table on a vector set (exact length valid, /25 beyond maxLength invalid, wrong origin invalid, uncovered prefix "not found" and still accepted).
- **Hostile input:** the AS-selector and prefix-length controls are fixed pickers; a typed prefix (optional field) goes through `parseCidr` and is refused unless it sits inside 203.0.113.0/24 or the documentation ranges, with a reason.
- **Honesty:** `Provenance` simulated (eight ASes; the real internet has many tens of thousands, from memory) and `from-memory` on the decision steps; "shown, not run: no BGP session exists here".
**Phone.** `GraphView` in a `WideRegion` (`minWidth` about 560, regions included). The candidate-path table stacks under it and keeps its sortable headers; the ROA toggles wrap.

**`wifi-shared-medium` · Wi-Fi & Shared Media**
Track `net` · *"Everyone talks on one wire, politely"* · Builds on: `ethernet-arp` · **[domain review]**
Moved up from Tier 2 (plan §1.1); `ethernet-arp` fences "Wi-Fi (moved to T3)". Backlog visual kept: CSMA/CA backoff and collisions.
**Spec.**
- **Scene 1, why not detect collisions.** A radio cannot listen while it transmits and two stations can be out of each other's range, so Wi-Fi tries to *avoid* collisions and infers loss from a missing ACK. A stepped timeline (sense idle for DIFS, pick a random backoff, count down only while idle, send, wait for ACK) contrasts with the old shared Ethernet (CSMA/CD) in one line.
- **Scene 2, the DCF contention window** on `K3-2 createTickSim` with an abstract **virtual slot**: each virtual slot is idle, one station sending (success) or several sending (collision). Stations 2-12 (slider) always have a frame (saturation). Each picks a backoff uniformly in 0..CW and decrements it only in idle slots. At 0 it sends. Success resets CW to CWmin; a collision (no ACK) sets `CW = min(2·(CW+1) - 1, CWmax)`. Constants `CW_MIN = 15`, `CW_MAX = 1023` (802.11 OFDM DCF defaults, from memory; verify against IEEE 802.11 and note EDCA access categories differ). A `GanttLanes` shows one lane per station (send bars, hatched waiting), collisions marked `--bad`.
  - Readouts: collision share of attempts, share of slots carrying a successful payload, per-station successes (a fairness bar row), mean contention window.
- **Scene 3, hidden node.** A 2D plane with an access point and two stations A and C that the learner drags. Radio range is a draggable-radius circle. "Hears" is computed from distance. A and C out of range of each other but both in range of the AP: their frames overlap at the AP and are lost, and neither sensed anything. A toggle enables **RTS/CTS**: the AP's CTS is heard by both and sets a network allocation vector (NAV) that silences the hidden station for the exchange. A frame-length slider shows the overhead trade-off: RTS/CTS costs a fixed number of slots and only pays when frames are long or hidden nodes exist.
- **Scene 4, one slow station.** Per-station data rate (6 or 54 Mbit/s, labelled illustrative); equal frame size means the slow station occupies the air much longer. Per-station airtime share and throughput are computed, showing that a station's slowness taxes everyone (the "rate anomaly", research finding of Heusse et al., 2003; verify).
**Out of scope.** PHY modulation and coding, OFDMA and MU-MIMO, WPA security, roaming, channel planning, rate adaptation (named). Real timings (SIFS, DIFS, preamble, ACK) are one illustrative chip, not modelled.
**Kit.** `createTickSim` (K3-2), `GanttLanes` with `barAt` (K3-10), `Slider`, `Toggle`, `Segmented`, `Plot2D` (`Points` draggable, `Curve` for the range circle sampled), `Bar` rows, `useScenePicker`, `createRecorder`, `core/random` `mulberry32`.
**AC.**
- **Watch:** a successful frame `--ok`, a collided frame `--bad`, a station counting down `--info` hatched (waiting, not blocked on I/O), a station that sent `--hi`, NAV-silenced `--violet`, out-of-range `--dim`. Station identity uses the plain tones (`amber`, `pink`, `sky`, `orange`), never `--ok`/`--bad`.
- **Misconception:** "Wi-Fi detects collisions like old Ethernet", and "ten stations each get a tenth of the throughput". Aggregate throughput *falls* as stations are added because collisions waste airtime. A quiz question targets each.
- **Correctness:**
  - Per-attempt collision probability from the saturated sim, for n = 2, 5, 10, matches the Bianchi fixed point (`τ = 2(1-2p) / ((1-2p)(W+1) + pW(1-(2p)^m))`, `p = 1-(1-τ)^(n-1)`, `W = 16`, `m = 6`) within 0.02 absolute over 200k virtual slots. Verify the formula against Bianchi (2000), "Performance analysis of the IEEE 802.11 DCF".
  - CW sequence is 15, 31, 63, ..., 1023, 1023. A station's backoff counter is unchanged across busy slots (a test freezes it). Exactly one counter reaching 0 is a success; two or more is a collision.
  - Hidden node: a collision occurs at the AP iff the two transmissions overlap in time and both stations are in AP range; with RTS/CTS on, no *data* frame collides on any of 200 seeds (RTS frames still can; the readout says so). Geometry is checked against a brute-force distance test.
  - Mixed rates: airtime share equals frame time over total, recomputed.
- **Hostile input:** stations 2-12 and frame length are sliders; a dragged station is clamped to the plane and positions are rounded; zero stations is not reachable. If total successes are 0 the efficiency stat shows "–", never 0%.
- **Honesty:** `Provenance` simulated (seed shown), `from-memory` on slot, CW and rate values. "Abstract slots: real Wi-Fi timing has SIFS, DIFS, preambles and ACKs".
**Phone.** `GanttLanes` in a `WideRegion` (`minWidth` about 520). The plane is a 340-wide canvas with draggable points that also move with the arrow keys; controls wrap.

**`congestion-control` · TCP Congestion Control**
Track `net` · *"Slow start, then back off"* · Builds on: `tcp-udp`; soft links to `latency-bandwidth` (BDP) and `queueing-theory` · **[domain review]**
Fence quoted: `latency-bandwidth` says "How TCP grows the window (slow start, congestion avoidance, loss reaction: T3 `congestion-control`). The window is a learner-set number here." `tcp-udp` shows the window collapsing to 1 on a timeout; this lesson shows that a duplicate-ACK loss halves instead, and what changes with the algorithm.
**Spec.**
- **Link model** (per-RTT, fluid, labelled): sender, one bottleneck link (capacity in segments per RTT unit derived from Mbit/s and base RTT, with a buffer of `B` segments), receiver. The bandwidth-delay product `BDP` is shown. Each round the sender sends `cwnd` segments; the standing queue is `max(0, cwnd - BDP)`; the measured RTT is `RTT0 + queue/rate`; a window above `BDP + B` overflows the buffer and the excess is lost. Controls: bandwidth, base RTT, buffer (in BDPs), a random non-congestive loss slider.
- **Scene 1, Reno.** Initial window 10 segments (RFC 6928, verify). Slow start doubles per round to `ssthresh` (initially unbounded), then congestion avoidance adds 1 per round. Loss detected by duplicate ACKs: `ssthresh = cwnd/2`, `cwnd = ssthresh` (fast retransmit and recovery). A timeout (a toggle: "whole window lost") sets `cwnd = 1` and restarts slow start (RFC 5681, verify). The `cwnd` sawtooth, queue depth and RTT draw on three stacked `Plot2D`s sharing the x axis, with the BDP and BDP+B lines.
- **Scene 2, three algorithms on the same link**, one chart each, same seed: Reno; **CUBIC** (`W(t) = C·(t-K)³ + Wmax`, `K = ∛(Wmax(1-β)/C)`, `C = 0.4`, `β = 0.7`, time from rounds × RTT; RFC 9438 or its predecessor RFC 8312, verify); **BBR** as a model-based sender (estimates bottleneck bandwidth as a windowed maximum of delivery rate and minimum RTT, paces at `gain × BtlBw`, `cwnd` capped near 2 × BDP; phases Startup with gain 2/ln 2, Drain, ProbeBW cycling gains 1.25, 0.75, then six rounds of 1; ProbeRTT named, not simulated). BBR numbers are BBRv1 from the 2016 ACM Queue paper (Cardwell et al.) and later versions differ: stated on screen. A buffer slider shows both failure shapes: shallow buffer (Reno and CUBIC lose often and under-use the link), deep buffer (they fill it and RTT balloons: bufferbloat), while BBR ignores loss and keeps the queue short.
- **Scene 3, fairness (the Chiu-Jain plot).** `Plot2D` with the two flows' rates on x and y, the efficiency line `x1 + x2 = capacity` and the fairness line `x1 = x2`. The learner drags the start point and picks the increase/decrease rule (AIAD, MIAD, MIMD, AIMD); the trajectory is computed. Only AIMD converges to the fair, efficient point.
- **Scene 4, loss is not always congestion.** Random loss from 0 to 2%: Reno's throughput collapses; a curve of average window vs loss rate is drawn against the Mathis relation (`window ≈ 1.22/√p` segments; verify against Mathis et al., 1997).
**Out of scope.** ECN, delay-based schemes other than BBR (named), TCP options (SACK, timestamps), RACK, pacing implementation, flow control (the receiver window `rwnd`; one chip: the sender uses `min(cwnd, rwnd)`), QUIC loss recovery (next lesson names it).
**Kit.** `Plot2D` (`Curve`, `VLine`, `HLine`, `Points` draggable, `Arrow`), K3-14 `timeFrames` for the stepped Reno story, `createTickSim` is not needed (per-round model, pure), `Slider`, `Segmented`, `Toggle`, `ChipButton`, `Stat` multi-line sub (K3-3), `core/random`, `useScenePicker`, `core/format`.
**AC.**
- **Watch:** `cwnd` `--hi`, queue `--info`, the BDP line `--ok`, BDP+B (loss threshold) `--bad` dashed, a loss event a `--bad` tick, slow start vs avoidance as two plain tints (`amber`, `sky`) on the x axis, a timeout `--orange` (the second failure kind), BBR phases as plain tints.
- **Misconception:** "congestion control is the receiver's job" (flow control is; the sender infers the network's limit from loss and delay), "a lost packet always means congestion" (scene 4), and "more bandwidth is always more throughput" (a long fat pipe needs a window of one BDP). A quiz question targets the first two.
- **Correctness:**
  - Reno on a lossless link with a buffer: the window never exceeds `BDP + B` plus one round of growth (checked every round), and the first loss comes at the round where `cwnd` first exceeds `BDP + B`, computed by hand for the default preset.
  - Mean `cwnd` under pure random loss (no buffer, no timeouts) matches `√(3/(2p))` within 15% for p = 1e-3 and 1e-4 over 200k rounds (derived from the AIMD sawtooth: a cycle carries 3W²/8 segments).
  - CUBIC: `W(0) = 0.7·Wmax`, `W(K) = Wmax`, and for `Wmax = 100`, `K = ∛75 = 4.217` s (hand-computed vector).
  - AIMD fairness: the gap `|x1 - x2|` is multiplied by exactly β at each decrease and unchanged by each increase; AIAD keeps the gap constant, MIMD preserves the ratio. Asserted on 200 seeded starts.
  - BBR: with a steady link, the bandwidth estimate converges to capacity and the queue stays below 1 BDP after Drain.
- **Hostile input:** all controls are sliders or pickers; the buffer cannot be 0 (minimum 1 segment, stated); bandwidth and RTT come with sane bounds and units; a window that would exceed the cap saturates and says so.
- **Honesty:** `Provenance` simulated (per-RTT fluid model; no ACK clocking, no delayed ACKs) and `from-memory` for IW10, β, `C` and the BBR gains; "BBRv1 shown; later versions changed the loss response".

**`quic` · QUIC & 0-RTT**

**`gpu-rendering-pipeline` · The GPU Rendering Pipeline & Shaders**
Track `web` · *"Vertices in, frames out"* · Builds on: `simd-gpu-parallelism`, `3d-transforms-projection` (same batch, earlier); soft link to `rasterization` · **[domain review]**
Fence quoted: `rasterization` leaves "Projection, shaders and the GPU pipeline stay in T3 `gpu-rendering-pipeline`". 2D top-left coverage and the z-buffer are taught there and only referred to here.
**Spec.**
- **Scene 1, the stages.** A cube (and an icosphere preset) flows through: input assembly (vertex and index buffers) → **vertex shader** (positions to clip space, passing attributes) → primitive assembly and clipping → rasterisation → **fragment shader** → depth test → blending → framebuffer. A stepper highlights one triangle through every stage. After rasterisation the covered pixels are drawn as fragments in a 24 by 18 `CellGrid`, each with its interpolated attributes on hover or focus.
- **Scene 2, an editable fragment shader.** Three fields (r, g, b) take an expression over named inputs `u`, `v` (surface coordinates), `nl` (the Lambert term N·L), `z` (depth, 0-1), `x`, `y` (screen, 0-1) and `bx`, `by`, `bz` (barycentric weights), with functions `sin cos abs min max clamp mix step smoothstep fract floor pow sqrt`. The evaluator is hand-written (tokeniser, precedence parser, tree walk) with a size cap and a depth cap. The image re-renders on change. Presets: flat colour, barycentric rainbow (`bx, by, bz`), Lambert, checker (`abs(step(0.5, fract(u*4)) - step(0.5, fract(v*4)))`), depth fog, toon (`floor(nl*3)/3`). A bad expression is refused with the reason and the previous image kept; a result that is NaN or infinite for a pixel draws a `--bad` hatch and the legend says why (for example `sqrt` of a negative).
- **Scene 3, perspective-correct interpolation.** A checker texture on a slanted quad, with the interpolation toggle **screen-linear** (wrong) vs **perspective-correct** (divide attributes by `w`, interpolate, divide back). The wrong one visibly bends the squares.
- **Scene 4, why order and early depth testing matter.** Several overlapping triangles, a draw order picker (back to front, front to back, shuffled) and an **early depth test** toggle. The readout counts **fragment shader invocations** (the cost) and shows that front to back with early-z runs fewer; the picture is identical for opaque triangles. A one-line chip says early-z is skipped when a shader writes depth or discards (from memory).
- **Scene 5, blending.** Two translucent triangles with `src-alpha, 1-src-alpha` blending: swapping the order changes the colour (blending is not commutative), numbers shown per pixel.
**Out of scope.** Real shader languages (GLSL, WGSL, HLSL), compute shaders, tessellation, geometry shaders, tiling and hierarchical-z, texture filtering, MSAA, what a warp or wavefront does (`simd-gpu-parallelism` owns it; one chip links back).
**Kit.** `core/raster3d` (`clipTriangle`, barycentric, z-buffer, `shade`), `core/vec`, `paintImage`, `CellGrid` (per-cell tone), `CodePanel` (the vertex and fragment stage listings, K3-6 min-width fix; the expression fields are `TextField`s), `TextField` + a local hand-written expression parser (a second lesson needing one would move it to `core/`), `ChipButton`, `Toggle`, `Segmented`, `useScenePicker`, `Stat`.
**AC.**
- **Watch:** the current stage `--hi`, stages already passed `--ok` ticks, data in flight between stages `--info`, a fragment that failed the depth test `--dim`, a fragment that passed `--ok`, NaN pixels `--bad` hatched, the vertex-stage and fragment-stage lanes as plain tones (`amber`, `pink`) so they never mean pass or fail.
- **Misconception:** "the GPU draws one triangle at a time, so order never affects cost" (fragment work depends on overdraw and draw order), and "a fragment is a pixel" (a fragment is a candidate for a pixel; several can compete). A quiz question targets each.
- **Correctness:**
  - Visibility: for the default cube and for 200 seeded random triangle soups, the z-buffer image equals an independent per-pixel reference (for each pixel centre, gather every covering triangle, pick the smallest depth); draw order does not change the opaque result.
  - Perspective-correct attributes: at every pixel, the interpolated `u` equals the `u` obtained by unprojecting the pixel centre, intersecting the triangle's plane and computing `u` from the world-space hit (1e-6). The screen-linear mode must *differ* on the slanted quad (a test, so the toggle is not decorative).

- **Hostile input:** expression fields cap at 120 characters and 60 tokens; unknown names, assignments, loops, strings and function calls outside the list are refused with the reason; division by zero and overflow follow IEEE (inf/NaN) and are drawn as said above; nothing is ever evaluated as JavaScript.
- **Honesty:** `Provenance` toy ("a teaching pipeline in software on a canvas; real GPUs add tiling, hierarchical depth tests and massive parallelism"), `from-memory` on the early-z caveat. The shader language is "a toy expression language, not GLSL".

**`fuzzing` · Fuzzing**

**`cache-stampede` · Cache Stampede & Thundering Herd**

**Spec.**

- **Scene 3, the broken singleflight.** A toggle **"release the in-flight marker before the cache is written"** reproduces a real race: the winner finishes, drops the marker, and the value lands a few milliseconds later; requests arriving in that gap start their own recompute. A time-ordered log shows it.

**`vpns-tunneling` · VPNs & Tunneling (IPsec, WireGuard)**

**Spec.**

A simulated TCP transfer: the handshake and small requests succeed, then full-size segments exceed the tunnel MTU and, with ICMP blocked and DF set, **silently vanish**: pings and small pages work, large transfers stall. Clamping the MSS (or allowing ICMP) fixes it. This is the classic "path MTU discovery black hole" symptom, shown by the model.

The learner sends, replays and reorders packets and watches the window bitmap.

**Kit.** `BitStrip` / `ByteGrid` (`fields`, `perRow={8}` on phones), `SequenceDiagram`, `core/ipv4` (`parseCidr`, `contains`, the longest-prefix helper), `.data-table` with K3-4 sortable header, `Slider`, `Segmented`, `Toggle` ×4, `TextField` (destination, via `parseIPv4`), `ChipButton`, `Stat` multi-line, `core/bits`, `Provenance`, `createRecorder`.
