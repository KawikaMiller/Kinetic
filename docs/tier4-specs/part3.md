**How the numbers in these specs were produced.** Every vector marked **[hand]** is worked in the spec with its arithmetic. Every number marked **[proto]** comes from a throwaway Node prototype I wrote while specifying (litmus explorer, Treiber-stack explorer, the guarded-command checker, ratchet with `node:crypto`, LWE, Shamir, Schnorr, quantiser, serving simulator). The prototypes are not committed and are **not oracles**: a builder re-derives every [proto] number with the real implementation and pins it in a test only after reading why it came out that way. Where the prototype and the spec's hand arithmetic agree, the spec says so. [node] marks a value taken from `node:crypto` output (P-256 and secp256k1 points, HKDF, HMAC), which is the oracle the tests will use anyway.

**House rules baked into every lesson below** (CLAUDE.md and `docs/qa-tier3-report.md`): every number on screen is computed from the model and no sentence states a figure the model did not produce; every "try this" entry names its scene and works from that scene's default state in written order (a test `try-this N:` per entry); every model has an independent oracle (a second formulation, `node:crypto`, brute force or exhaustion); a broken mode fails for the stated reason and not trivially everywhere (AC-0/T4 #29); hidden controls feed no number (a control that changes a figure is visible and labelled); scenes are keyed (`useScenePicker`), generated ids key React lists, and no fresh array goes into `usePlayer`, `useTickSim` or an intro key (AC-0/T4 #37); every control is tested at its minimum, maximum and one beyond; `npm run sweep` is clean at 1440 and 390 (AC-0/T4 #38); absolutes are scoped ("in this model", "in the N states explored"); every fact from memory wears a `from-memory` chip; security lessons are defensive and payload-free, toy parameters are labelled toy and never called secure, and fake secrets come from `core/fakeSecret`.

**Quiz convention** (same as Tier 3): each question lists the correct option first as it will sit in `answer: 0`; K-2 enforces the 0.75-1.35x length band and that the correct option is strictly the longest in at most half the questions (aim: at most one in three). Distractor notes say which misconception each wrong option stands for, so a reviewer can check none is a straw man. Options are written to similar lengths on purpose. **The distractor lists below are seeds, not final text:** the builder writes each distractor as a full clause of about the same length as the correct option (within the 0.75-1.35x band), and where a correct option here is longer than 1.35x the mean of its distractors the builder shortens it or lengthens them; no more than one question in three may have the correct option strictly longest.

**Decisions this part makes that other documents left open.**
1. **`relaxed` mode of K4-3 is KEPT, scoped to what message passing needs** (memory-ordering-atomics). On TSO the message-passing outcome is already forbidden, so on `sc` and `tso` alone release/acquire annotations change nothing visible and the lesson cannot show why they exist. A view-based weak model (about 60 lines, prototyped: [proto] it forbids MP only when the flag store is release **and** the flag load is acquire) is the smallest thing that makes them visible. Load buffering, IRIW, 2+2W, out-of-thin-air values and `consume` stay out; they are named on screen as "not modelled". If K4-3 ships without `relaxed`, scene 2 of memory-ordering-atomics degrades to "on TSO this is already safe", acquire/release become words only, and the lesson loses its second half: raise it as a kit blocker, do not quietly cut.
2. **`lock-free-cas` does not run on `core/memModel`.** The Litmus language has no loops, no indirect addressing (`h.next` where `h` came from a register) and no conditional retry, so a Treiber stack cannot be written in it. The CAS counter and the stack are lesson-local pure state machines over `core/explore` (K4-2), exactly as prototyped. K4-3's `cas` instruction therefore has **no user in this batch**; keep it only if another lesson wants it, otherwise drop it from K4-3 (kit owner's call).
3. **The LLM serving simulator is lesson-local** (see `llm-serving`). The plan's §6.2 row "LLM serving simulator" as kit and technical §4.1's "kept local" disagree; this part sides with technical §4.1, because the only thing `fine-tuning-quantization` shares with it is one line, `bytes = parameters x bits / 8`.
4. **Reviewer split.** B2 and B7 (two reviewers each): reviewer 1 takes the first two lessons, reviewer 2 the last two. B6 (two reviewers, payload-free): per crypto lesson, **reviewer A recomputes every vector independently** (never from the lesson's own output: `node:crypto`, a second implementation, or by hand), **reviewer B reads only for overclaiming and payload-freeness** against the checklist in each lesson's Review split. The batch has two reviewers for five lessons, so each plays both roles: reviewer 1 is A on `elliptic-curves` and `post-quantum-crypto` and B on the other three; reviewer 2 is the reverse. Every lesson gets one A read and one B read, never both from the same person.

**`memory-ordering-atomics` · Memory Ordering & Atomics** **[domain review]**
Track `os` · *"Your CPU reorders your code"* · Builds on: `race-conditions`, `cache-coherence`, `cpu-pipelining` · priority important
Fences quoted. From `race-conditions`: *"Atomic operations (fetch_add, AtomicInteger) do read-modify-write as one step."* This lesson never uses `fetch_add` as the fix for a lost update. From the `cache-coherence` spec (T3-B23): its bus transactions are atomic and it states *"real cores reorder"* in one caption. This lesson starts there, and says first and on screen: **coherence is per location, ordering is across locations**. A coherent machine can still show you a result no interleaving of your lines explains.

**Spec.**
- **Rules panel (always visible, collapsible on phones).** Three models, each with its rules as a short list, a `Provenance simulated` chip ("a model of memory ordering, not your CPU"), and a "does not cover" line. *Sequential (SC):* one step at a time, every thread's instructions in program order, a store is visible to all at once. *x86-style (TSO):* each core has a FIFO store buffer; a store enters it and drains to memory later, in order; a load reads the core's own newest buffered store to that location if there is one, else memory; a full fence waits for the buffer to drain. *Weak (release/acquire):* every location has a history of writes; each thread has a view per location (the oldest write it may still read); a plain load may read any write at or after its view; a release store records the writer's whole view with the write; an acquire load that reads it merges that view; a full fence advances the thread's view to the newest write of every location. Does not cover (printed on screen): load buffering, IRIW and multi-copy atomicity, out-of-thin-air values, `consume`, compiler reordering (named in words as a *separate* source of reordering), and every detail of any real ISA. "seq_cst" access in the weak model is modelled as a full fence before the access: stronger than C++ in corner cases, the same on the tests shown (uncertain, verify).
- **Scene 1 · Store buffering (SB).** Preset litmus test `T0: x = 1; r0 = y` / `T1: y = 1; r1 = x`, `x = y = 0` at start. Layout: two thread cards (instruction list with the next instruction ringed `--hi`, a store buffer drawn as a FIFO row of cells, two registers), a shared memory strip (`x`, `y`). Model picker (`Segmented`: SC, x86-style, Weak). **Default state: SC.** Edit controls (no free text, bounded): after each instruction a "fence" toggle (full fence; at most one per gap), and in the Weak model an order tag per memory access (`relaxed`, `acquire` on loads, `release` on stores, `seq_cst`).
  - **Outcome table (computed, every outcome enumerated):** one row per final `(r0, r1)` that is reachable in the chosen model, each with its **shortest schedule** (a `TracePlayer` button "show it" replays store, buffer, drain and load steps), plus a row for every outcome that is **not** reachable, dimmed, reading "not reachable: all N states of this model explored". The state count and `truncated` flag are printed ("43 states, exhaustive"). Rows reachable only because of the model's relaxation (compared with SC) are marked.
  - **Manual mode:** buttons "step T0", "step T1", "drain T0's buffer", "drain T1's buffer" let the learner try to reach `r0 = r1 = 0` by hand. A button is disabled only when the model disables it, with the reason in text ("T1's fence waits for its buffer to drain"). Under SC the learner cannot reach it, and after trying the page says why in numbers: "6 interleavings of 4 steps, none gives 0 and 0".
- **Scene 2 · Message passing (MP).** `T0: x = 1 (data); y = 1 (flag)` / `T1: r1 = y; r2 = x`. The surprising outcome is `r1 = 1, r2 = 0` (flag seen, data not). The same table and controls. The verdict line is derived from the table: forbidden in SC and TSO ("x86-style stores never pass each other"), reachable in Weak with plain accesses, **still reachable if only the store is release**, **still reachable if only the load is acquire**, forbidden when both are, and forbidden with a full fence on each side. A caption says code that works on x86-style ordering can break on weaker ISAs (from memory, chipped).
- **Scene 3 · Where it bites, and what survives.** Two presets on the same machinery. *Entering a critical section Dekker-style:* each thread sets its own flag, then checks the other's; both enter exactly when the SB outcome `0, 0` occurs. Under TSO it is reachable; **a fence in one thread only does not remove it** (computed: still reachable), fences in both do. *Coherence (CoRR):* `T0: x = 1; x = 2` / `T1: r1 = x; r2 = x`: the outcome `r1 = 2, r2 = 1` is **not reachable in any of the three models**, with the line "coherence is per location: even the weak model never reads x backwards".
- **Closing panel.** "Why hardware does this": store buffers hide the wait for a line (link `cache-coherence` by id for ownership transfer, `cpu-pipelining` in words). Hardware fences cost cycles (named, no number).

**Out of scope.** MESI and false sharing (`cache-coherence`), `fetch_add` and mutexes as fixes for lost updates (`race-conditions`), CAS loops and ABA (`lock-free-cas`, next), the full C++ memory model, language volatile semantics, ARM and POWER instruction-level details (named), compiler reordering (one sentence).
**Kit.** `core/memModel` (K4-3: `outcomes(l, model, budget)` returning outcomes with `example` paths, `states`, `truncated`), `core/explore` through it, `components/TracePlayer` (K4-2, builds its frames internally: AC-0/T4 #37), `CellGrid` (store buffers, memory strip), `CodePanel` (instruction lists, `stateOf` for the next instruction), `Segmented`, `Toggle`, `Stat`, `Provenance`, `useScenePicker`.
**AC.**
- **Watch:** next instruction `--hi`; a store sitting in a buffer (not yet visible to the other core) `--info`; a value committed to memory `muted`; a fence and an order tag `--violet` (structural); an outcome that SC cannot produce `--bad`; an outcome SC also produces `muted`; a not-reachable row `--dim`. State the map in `watch`.
- **Misconception:** "coherent caches mean every core sees writes in the same order" and "this only matters on ARM". Quiz questions 1 and 3.
- **Correctness (independent oracles; AC-0/T4 #31, #28):**
  - **SC** outcomes equal the outcomes of enumerating all interleavings of instruction steps (a second implementation: plain recursion over thread positions, no explorer). **[hand]** SB under SC: with a: `x=1`, b: `r0=y`, c: `y=1`, d: `r1=x` (a before b, c before d), the 6 interleavings give abcd (0,1), acbd (1,1), acdb (1,1), cabd (1,1), cadb (1,1), cdab (1,0): outcome counts (0,1):1, (1,0):1, (1,1):4, (0,0):0. Check each by reading the order. [proto] agrees.
  - **TSO from a second formulation:** for programs where no thread reads a location it has a pending store to, TSO outcomes equal the union of SC outcomes over every program obtained by swapping a store past a later load of a **different** location in the same thread (a fence blocks the swap). SB: swaps give four programs, union `{01, 10, 11, 00}`. MP has no store-then-load pair, so TSO = SC. The fixture set is restricted to this fragment and says so in a comment; outside it (forwarding cases) the oracle is not used.
  - **Containment (property test, uncertain, verify):** over 300 seeded random programs (2 threads, at most 3 instructions each, 2 locations, values 0 to 2) outcomes(SC) is a subset of outcomes(TSO) is a subset of outcomes(Weak). If a counterexample appears, decide whether the model or the claim is wrong before pinning anything.
  - **Counterfactuals (they must fail):** a TSO that drains on every store (SC in disguise) fails "SB reaches 0,0"; a model that ignores fences fails "SB with fences in both threads cannot reach 0,0"; a weak model whose acquire load does not merge the writer's view fails "MP with release and acquire forbids 1,0".
  - **Published expectations, typed from memory, reviewer verifies each cell (see From memory vs verified):** SB: SC `{01,10,11}`, TSO adds `00`, with a full fence in both threads `00` is forbidden everywhere. MP: `1,0` forbidden on SC and TSO, allowed on weaker ISAs, forbidden with release/acquire or fences. CoRR: `2,1` forbidden everywhere. [proto] reachable counts, keyed on (pcs, memory, buffers, registers): SB 14/43/18 states (SC/TSO/Weak), MP 13/23/14, MP with release+acquire 13 (Weak), CoRR 19/31/19, SB with fences both 23/32/31, SB with a fence in one thread only: `00` still reachable in TSO and Weak. State counts depend on the state key and are a regression pin only; **outcome sets are key-independent** and are what the tests assert first.
  - **Manipulations (AC-0/T4 #30; tests named `manipulation N:`):** (1) removing one fence of two leaves `00` reachable, and the page says "one fence is not enough: the other thread's store can still sit in its buffer"; (2) tagging only the flag store `release` still shows `1,0` in Weak, with the refusal-style line "release alone does not order the reader".
- **Hostile input:** no typed text. Fence toggles and tags are bounded to the model; a tag the chosen model ignores (anything in SC or TSO) is shown disabled with "this model orders every access anyway". The explorer budget is fixed (250,000 states); if `truncated` ever becomes true the table says "no more outcomes found in the first N states, not exhaustive" and never "not reachable".
- **Honesty:** persistent `Provenance simulated` ("a model of memory ordering, not your CPU: it says what the model allows, not what a given chip does"); `from-memory` on every named real-world fact; no sentence of the form "this is what happens on x86". Outcome tables are labelled "allowed by the model", not "observed".

**Try this** (4 entries; each names its scene first, so each works from the default state; tests `try-this 1..4:`).
1. Open "Store buffering" (default SC): three outcomes, and `r0 = 0, r1 = 0` listed as not reachable ("all 14 states explored" [proto]). Switch to x86-style: a fourth outcome appears; press "show it": both stores sit in their buffers while both loads read 0 from memory.

**Quiz** (5 questions; the misconception ones are 1 and 3).
1. *(misconception)* Two cores each store to one variable and then load the other core's variable, and both loads read 0. Which explanation fits the lesson's model? **Correct:** each store was still waiting in its own core's store buffer when the other core loaded from memory. Distractors: the caches were incoherent, so each core kept a stale copy of the other variable (the coherence-equals-ordering misconception); one of the stores was lost on the way to memory; both loads ran before the program started. Note: the distractor about a lost store is a straw man unless it is as specific as the correct one, so write it as "was dropped before it reached memory" at equal length.
2. A writer stores data and then sets a flag with a release store. The reader loads the flag with a plain relaxed load, sees 1, then reads the data. In the weak model can it read the old data? **Correct:** yes: release on the writer is not enough, the reader's load must be acquire to take over the writer's view. Distractors: no, release orders everything the writer did before the flag; no, because x86 never reorders stores; yes, but only if the data is stored with a fence in front. Note: the third distractor is the "TSO-only" misconception.
3. *(misconception)* Which of these can never happen, even in the weak model? **Correct:** a thread reads the new value of one variable and then, later in the same thread, an older value of the same variable. Distractors: a thread sees a flag set and the data it guarded still unset; two threads both read 0 after each writing 1 to the other's variable; a store becomes visible to one core before another. Note: tests coherence versus ordering.
4. Only one of the two threads in the store-buffering test has a fence between its store and its load. Is `0, 0` still possible in the x86-style model? **Correct:** yes: the other thread's store can still sit in its buffer while its load reads memory. Distractors: no, a fence anywhere flushes both buffers; no, because the unfenced thread's load runs second; yes, but only on the weak model. Note: option 2's "flushes both" is the common over-reading of a fence.
5. The page says `0, 0` is reachable in its x86-style model. What may you conclude about your laptop? **Correct:** nothing measured: it is what a model with store buffers allows, which real documented memory models approximate. Distractors: your laptop will print 0 and 0 about half the time; your laptop reorders every pair of stores; your laptop's compiler will always produce it. Note: guards the AC-0/T4 #31 honesty line.

**`lock-free-cas` · Compare-and-Swap & Lock-Free Structures** **[domain review]**
Track `os` · *"Concurrency without locks"* · Builds on: `memory-ordering-atomics` (earlier in this batch), `race-conditions`, `thread-pools-work-stealing` · priority nice
Fences quoted. `thread-pools-work-stealing` keyIdea 2: *"A thief takes the oldest task from the front of a deque ... the owner works the newest end"*: the work-stealing deque is not rebuilt here. `priority-inversion` and `race-conditions` own locks and the mutex as a fix. `cache-coherence` owns why a contended line bounces: named in one caption, linked by id. `race-conditions` quiz says 18 of 20 orderings of the *six-step* read-add-write lose an update; this lesson folds the add into a local step, so its counts (4 of 6) are for a **different step granularity** and the page says so.

**Spec.** All schedules, heaps and counts come from lesson-local pure state machines driven by `core/explore` (K4-2) and shown in `TracePlayer`. The memory model is sequentially consistent (the page says: "ordering of the CAS itself is a `memory-ordering-atomics` question; here every step is atomic and in order").
- **Scene 1 · The CAS loop.** A shared counter `x` and, per thread, `old = load x; if cas(x, old, old + 1) done else retry`. Thread count 2 or 3 (`Segmented`), one increment each. A **schedule editor**: a lane per thread with its next step (load or CAS), and buttons "run T1 next" etc.; or presets (fair alternation, "T1 stalls after its load", "T2 sneaks in"). Each step shows `x`, each thread's remembered `old`, and a failed CAS in `--bad` with "x is now 1, you expected 0: retry". Counters: successful CAS, failed CAS, retries.
  - **Exhaustive table (computed):** all executions for 2 threads (6) and 3 threads (498 [proto]) with the number having 0, 1, 2, 3 retries. **[hand]** 2 threads: the first-attempt interleavings of L1 C1 L2 C2 are 6; L1 C1 L2 C2 and L2 C2 L1 C1 retry nothing (2), the other four (L1 L2 C1 C2, L1 L2 C2 C1, L2 L1 C1 C2, L2 L1 C2 C1) fail exactly one CAS and retry once: 4. The final value is 2 in all 6 [proto 3 threads: executions with 0/1/2/3 retries are 6/60/216/216, final 3 every time]. **The link to `race-conditions`:** the 4 interleavings that retry are exactly the 4 where an unlocked read-add-write loses an update (side-by-side column, computed from the unlocked model: for 3 threads the unlocked version keeps all 3 updates in 6 of 90 first-attempt interleavings, the same 6 that retry nothing). A failed CAS is the update the unlocked version would have lost, noticed in time.
- **Scene 2 · A lock-free stack and ABA.** Treiber stack: `push(n): do { h = head; n.next = h } while (!cas(head, h, n))`; `pop: do { h = head; nx = h.next } while (!cas(head, h, nx))`. A heap of three nodes **A, B, C**, a **free list** (an allocator that hands back the most recently freed node first: stated as a model choice, common in real allocators, from memory), and per-thread registers `r1, r2` for popped nodes. Initial stack `head -> A -> B -> C`.
  - **Thread programs (bounded editor):** up to 5 operations per thread from `pop`, `push rK`, `free rK`, `new` (allocate from the free list and push); an op that uses an empty register is refused with "r2 is empty: pop first". Presets: **ABA via reuse** (`T1: pop`; `T2: pop, pop, free r2, free r1, new`), **ABA by re-push** (`T1: pop`; `T2: pop, pop, push r1`), "two poppers".
  - **Every step is one atomic action:** load head; load `h.next`; CAS head; allocator call; store `n.next`. The learner interleaves by hand (stage of buttons) or presses **"find the corruption"**, which runs `explore` and replays the shortest path.
  - **Invariant (checked after every step):** the stack from `head` is acyclic; every node is in exactly one place (on the stack, on the free list, retired, or held by a thread); no node on the stack is also free, retired or held; none is lost. First failing check names the node: "B is on the stack and on the free list".
  - **Mode picker (`Segmented`):** *plain CAS*; *tagged head* (the head is a pair (node, counter); every successful CAS increments the counter; a CAS must match both); *hazard pointers* (a thread publishes the node it is about to read, re-checks `head`, and `free` of a published node is deferred; modelled with a retired list scanned at each `free`; the page names epoch-based reclamation in one sentence and does not model it).
  - **Results [proto, keyed on the whole heap state; pin the builder's own counts]:** *ABA via reuse*, plain: violation at **depth 15** (T1 loads head=A, loads A.next=B, stalls; T2 pops A, pops B, frees B, frees A (free list now A then B), allocates A back and pushes it; T1's CAS(head, A, B) succeeds because head is A again: head becomes B, B is on the free list, A is lost), 165 states seen. Tagged: **166 states, no violation, exhaustive**. Hazard pointers: **611 states, no violation, exhaustive**. With a third thread that also pops: plain violation at the same depth 15, tagged 2,847 states exhaustive, hazard 26,706 states exhaustive (all under the 250,000 budget). *ABA by re-push* (T2 pops A and B and pushes A straight back, never freeing it): plain violates at depth 13 ("B is on the stack and held by a thread"), **tagged passes (136 states), hazard pointers do not** (violation at depth 19): hazard pointers protect *reclamation*, not a program that recycles a node itself, and the page says so. A note on tag width: a counter of k bits wraps after 2^k successful CASes, so a tag narrows the window and a wide tag (or a double-width CAS) closes it for practical purposes (from memory, verify).
- **Scene 3 · Lock-free is not wait-free.** Two threads incrementing a counter. A lock-based counter with a thread **suspended while holding the lock**: after N=20 steps the other thread has completed 0 increments ("did not complete within 20 steps": AC-0/T3 #23 wording, never "forever"). The CAS counter under the same suspension: the other thread finishes. Then the **adversary scene:** an adversary schedule makes T2 complete a full load and CAS between T1's load and T1's CAS, each round: in 12 rounds T1 failed 12 times and completed 0 increments while T2 completed 12 (some thread always succeeded: lock-free; T1 got nothing: not wait-free). A `fetch_add` counter finishes T1 in one step on the same schedule (wait-free). Definitions are written as the three guarantees a reader can check against the runs; obstruction-free is named only.

**Quiz** (5 questions; misconception ones 1 and 2).
1. *(misconception)* The checker says "holds in all 20 reachable states". What has been shown? **Correct:** the property holds in every state of this small model, which is not a proof about the real program built from it. Distractors: the real implementation is free of races; no schedule of the real program can deadlock; the property holds for any number of processes.
2. *(misconception)* How does a model checker differ from random testing? **Correct:** it covers every reachable state of the model, so a violation has a shortest path, but only for the model's size. Distractors: it runs the real binary many more times; it proves the property for any number of processes; it shrinks a random input until the bug is small. Note: the third is `property-based-testing`'s idea.
3. Removing `turn == me` from the wait guard produces what? **Correct:** a deadlock: both processes raise their flags and wait on each other, while mutual exclusion still holds. Distractors: a violation of mutual exclusion in 6 steps; a state space that never ends; nothing, since the flag already says enough.
4. Why is the atomic lock's state count `2^N + N x 2^(N-1)`? **Correct:** with the lock free each process is idle or waiting; with it held one process is critical and the others idle or waiting. Distractors: each process doubles the number of locks; every process has 3 states independently (3^N: contradicted by the lock); the count is measured, not derived.
5. A counterexample is the shortest path to a bad state. Why is it useful beyond saying "unsafe"? **Correct:** it is a concrete schedule that can be replayed as a test against the real code. Distractors: it shows which line to delete; it proves the fix works; it lists every bad state.

- **Scene 3 · A toy lattice scheme (noise is the point).** A Regev-style toy over `Z_q` with **`q = 98`** (not prime on purpose: `q / 4 = 24.5` is never an integer, so the decoding rule has no ties; real schemes use specific primes, and ML-KEM's modulus is named in the sizes table below). Controls (`Segmented`/`Slider`/`SeedField`): dimension `n` 2-8 (default 4), samples `m` 4-16 (default 8), noise bound `B` 0-15 (default 2), seed. The page generates a secret `s in Z_q^n`, public rows `a_i` and `b_i = <a_i, s> + e_i mod q` with `e_i` uniform in `[-B, B]`. **Encrypt a bit** `mu`: pick a random subset `S` of the rows (each in with probability 1/2), `u = sum a_i`, `v = sum b_i + 49 mu mod q`. **Decrypt**: `d = v - <u, s> mod q`; the bit is 1 iff `d` is strictly closer to 49 than to 0 (distance taken mod q). Track `sec` · *"Any 3 of 5 keys open the vault"* · Builds on: `modular-arithmetic`, `randomness-csprng`, `secrets-management` · priority nice
Fences quoted. `secrets-management` keyIdea 4: *"If a secret leaks, rotate it immediately."* and `envelope-encryption-kms` (key wrapping, crypto-shredding) own storing and wrapping keys; `randomness-csprng` owns where random numbers must come from. This lesson owns **splitting one secret into shares so that `k` of `n` rebuild it and `k - 1` reveal nothing**: Shamir's scheme over a small prime field. The fields are toys and labelled so; **no real key material is ever split** (the secret is a number or a lesson-authored fake).

**Spec.**
- **Scene 1 · A polynomial hides the secret.** Field `GF(7)` (default) or `GF(5)`/`GF(11)` (`Segmented`), threshold `k` = 2 or 3. The learner sets the secret `s` (stepper) and the random coefficients (steppers, or "draw them" from the seeded generator); the page plots the polynomial `f(x) = s + a1 x (+ a2 x^2)` over the field as dots on a `p x p` grid (`CellGrid`), the secret as the dot at `x = 0`, and the shares at `x = 1..5`. The learner **ticks which shares an adversary holds**. Computed by exact enumeration of every coefficient tuple: for each candidate secret `s'`, **how many polynomials of degree at most `k - 1` through the held shares have `f(0) = s'`**. With `k - 1` shares each of the `p` candidates has **exactly 1** (seven equal bars, "every secret is equally possible"); with `k` shares exactly one polynomial exists overall and the secret is certain. The prior is stated: coefficients uniform.
  - **[hand] vectors in GF(7):** `k = 2`, `s = 3`, `a1 = 2`: `f(x) = 3 + 2x`, shares `(1, 5), (2, 0), (3, 2), (4, 4), (5, 6)` (e.g. `f(2) = 7 = 0`). Reconstruct from shares 1 and 3: `L1(0) = (0 - 3)/(1 - 3) = 3/2 = 3 x 4 = 12 = 5` (since `2^-1 = 4`), `L3(0) = (0 - 1)/(3 - 1) = -1/2 = -4 = 3`; `s = 5 x 5 + 3 x 2 = 31 = 3 mod 7`. `k = 3`, `f = 2 + 3x + x^2`: shares `(1, 6), (2, 5), (3, 6), (4, 2), (5, 0), (6, 0)`. The two shares `(1, 6), (2, 5)` are consistent with **every** secret `0..6`, one polynomial each: `s = 0` with `(a1, a2) = (6, 0)`, 1 with (1, 4), **2 with (3, 1)** (the real one), 3 with (5, 5), 4 with (0, 2), 5 with (2, 6), 6 with (4, 3) [proto, each satisfies `s + a1 + a2 = 6` and `s + 2 a1 + 4 a2 = 5` mod 7: check `s = 3`: `3 + 5 + 5 = 13 = 6`, `3 + 10 + 20 = 33 = 5` ok].
  - **Perfect secrecy, labelled and checked.** `ClaimLabel kind="theorem"`: "with uniformly random coefficients, any `k - 1` shares are independent of the secret: argued, not run here for large fields". Exhaustive **for GF(7), `k = 3`**: all 15 pairs of x-values (from 1..6) times all 49 pairs of y-values (**735** share pairs); for each, each of the 7 candidates has exactly 1 consistent polynomial (5,145 checks, printed). Also `k = 2`: all 6 x-values times 7 y-values (42 single shares), each candidate exactly 1.
- **Scene 2 · Share a byte (GF(257), 9 bits).** `p = 257` (prime, so every byte 0..255 is a field element), `(k, n)` from {(2, 3), (3, 5), (4, 6)}. The secret is a byte (typed `0..255`) or a short **word of at most 6 printable ASCII characters** (one polynomial per byte, same x-values; a lesson-authored fake, with "never type a real secret"). "Share" draws coefficients from the seeded generator and shows each share as `(x, y)` (hex per byte for a word). The learner **selects any subset** of shares and presses Reconstruct: with at least `k` shares the page shows Lagrange interpolation at 0 with every basis value and the sum; with fewer it **refuses** ("2 shares of a 3-of-5 scheme: every one of the 257 secrets fits equally well") but offers "interpolate anyway", which fits the unique line through the two points and reads its value at 0: **a wrong secret with probability 256/257**, shown against the truth.
  - **[hand] vector, `(k, n) = (3, 5)`, secret 42, `a1 = 100`, `a2 = 57` (mod 257):** `f(1) = 42 + 100 + 57 = 199`; `f(2) = 42 + 200 + 228 = 470 = 470 - 257 = 213`; `f(3) = 42 + 300 + 513 = 855 = 855 - 771 = 84`; `f(4) = 42 + 400 + 912 = 1354 = 1354 - 1285 = 69`; `f(5) = 42 + 500 + 1425 = 1967 = 1967 - 1799 = 168`. Shares `(1,199), (2,213), (3,84), (4,69), (5,168)`. Lagrange coefficients for x = {1, 2, 3} at 0: `3`, `-3`, `1` (`(0-2)(0-3)/((1-2)(1-3)) = 6/2`; `(0-1)(0-3)/((2-1)(2-3)) = 3/-1`; `(0-1)(0-2)/((3-1)(3-2)) = 2/2`); `s = 3 x 199 - 3 x 213 + 84 = 597 - 639 + 84 = 42`. Shares {2, 4, 5} also give 42; **all `C(5, 3) = 10` subsets give 42** (asserted). Two shares `(1, 199), (2, 213)`: slope 14, intercept `199 - 14 = 185`: the "interpolate anyway" answer is **185, not 42**.
- **Scene 3 · Where it goes wrong (the failing states).** (a) **A corrupted share rebuilds a wrong secret silently:** change share 3 from 84 to 85 and reconstruct from {1, 2, 3}: the result is **43** with no error (the error is multiplied by `L3 = 1`), because Shamir's scheme has no integrity check; verifiable secret sharing is named only. (b) **The same x twice** is refused ("two shares with the same x are one point"). (c) **x = 0** is refused ("x = 0 is the secret itself"). (d) **A bad coefficient source** (`Segmented`: uniform / "only 0 or 1"): in GF(7), `k = 3`, secret 2 and `a = (1, 1)`, so `f = 2 + x + x^2` and the shares `(1, 4), (2, 1)`; under the uniform source all 7 secrets stay possible, under the "0 or 1" source **exactly one** candidate (the true secret, 2) is consistent with the support: `(0,0)` would need `s = 4` and then `4 + 0 + 0 = 4 != 1`; `(0,1)` needs 3 and gives `0 != 1`; `(1,0)` needs 3 and gives `5 != 1`; only `(1,1)` fits. The page computes "secrets with non-zero probability: 1 of 7" from the source's support, and says why **the scheme's secrecy needs uniformly random coefficients** (`randomness-csprng`; seeded here only for reproducibility, "a real system uses a CSPRNG").
- **The honest scale line.** Guess chance from `k - 1` shares: `1/7` per guess (GF(7)), `1/257` (GF(257), a byte); for a 256-bit secret over a field of about 2^256 elements it is about `2^-256` (computed `power` display). Real uses are named with chips: some secrets-management products split an unseal or master key with Shamir shares; hardware-module and wallet backup schemes use threshold quorums (`from-memory`, no product details); real implementations commonly work over `GF(2^8)` bytewise, which is why this lesson's prime field is a readability choice.

**Out of scope.** KMS key wrapping and shredding (`envelope-encryption-kms`), vault operation and unseal procedures (`secrets-management`, named only), threshold signatures and multi-party computation (named), verifiable and proactive sharing (named), finite-field theory beyond the prime field, sharing real keys.
**Kit.** `core/field` (K4-6: `Fp` add, mul, inverse, interpolation helpers), `core/numberTheory` (`modInverse`), `core/random` (`mulberry32`), `core/fakeSecret` (the word mode's sample text and any credential-looking fake), `CellGrid` (field grid and shares), `Plot2D` (the candidate counts as `Bars`), `Segmented`, `Slider`/stepper, `TextField` + `parseBoundedInt`, `ChipButton`, `ClaimLabel` (theorem, K4-8), `SeedField`, `Stat`, `Provenance`, `useScenePicker`. Local `shamir.ts` (split, reconstruct by Lagrange, and the independent solve below).
**AC.**
- **Watch:** the secret (dot at x = 0 and its bar) `--hi`; a share held by the adversary `--bad`, a share held by a custodian `--info`; a candidate secret that is still possible `--info`, the true secret once certain `--ok`; the reconstructed polynomial `--violet`; a refused action `--bad`; a bar of equal candidates `muted`.
- **Misconception:** "k - 1 shares leak a bit of the secret" and "a share that is wrong is detected". Quiz 1 and 3.
- **Correctness (independent oracles, exhaustion):**
  - **Exhaustive secrecy** on GF(7) as above (735 pairs, 42 single shares, every candidate exactly 1); also GF(5) `k = 3` over all pairs, so the claim is not tuned to p = 7.
  - **Second formulation of reconstruction:** Lagrange interpolation at 0 equals the solution of the Vandermonde system mod p by Gaussian elimination (`core/field`) for 1,000 seeded `(p, k, n, secret, coefficients, subset)` cases; all `C(n, k)` subsets rebuild the secret (n = 5, k = 3: 10; n = 6, k = 4: 15); `k - 1` shares interpolated give a different value on at least 98% of 1,000 seeded GF(257) cases (expected 99.6%, the rest coincidences; the rate is printed, not asserted equal).
  - Hand vectors above (`(1,5),(2,0),...`; `(1,199)...`; the coefficient values 3, -3, 1; the sum 42; the corrupted 43; the 185); secret 0 and secret 255 and `k = n` (all shares needed) and `k = 1` (the secret is every share, which the page labels "a threshold of 1 hides nothing").
  - **Bad-source computation:** the number of candidate secrets with non-zero posterior equals the size of the set `{ y1 - a1 x1 - a2 x1^2 }` over the source's support filtered by the other shares, recomputed by brute force over the support; for the hand case it is 1 of 7, for the uniform source 7 of 7.
  - **Manipulations (`manipulation N:`):** (1) choose 2 of 3 shares in a 3-of-5 and read the refusal text, then "interpolate anyway" and see a wrong number; (2) corrupt one share and reconstruct: the result is 43, not 42, with no error; (3) switch to the 0-or-1 coefficient source and see "1 of 7" for the hand case.
- **Hostile input:** the secret is `parseBoundedInt` 0..255 (decimals, `1e3`, negatives and empty refused with the stated message) or a word of at most 6 printable ASCII characters (anything else refused with the reason); `k` and `n` from presets; share x-values 1..n, never 0 and never repeated; no pasted shares.
- **Honesty:** persistent `Provenance toy` ("GF(7) is 3 bits and GF(257) is 9 bits: a toy field; a real vault uses a large field or bytes over GF(2^8), a CSPRNG, and authenticated shares"), the theorem chip, `from-memory` on product and implementation claims, "nothing here splits a real key".

**Quiz** (4 questions; misconception questions 1 and 3).
1. *(misconception)* In a 3-of-5 scheme an adversary holds two shares. What do they learn about the secret? **Correct:** nothing: every possible secret is equally consistent with them. Distractors: its size; whether it is even or odd; about a third of it.
2. Which three shares of a 3-of-5 scheme rebuild the secret? **Correct:** any three of the five. Distractors: only the first three issued; any three that include share 1; any three whose x-values are consecutive. Note: all distractors are parallel "restricted subset" claims, so the correct option is the unrestricted one and not the only one with a clause.
3. *(misconception)* One of three shares used in a reconstruction was mistyped. What happens? **Correct:** a wrong secret comes out with no error, since the scheme has no integrity check. Distractors: reconstruction fails with an error; the mistyped share is detected and dropped; the secret is rebuilt correctly from the other two.
4. Why does the "0 or 1 only" coefficient source break the secrecy of two shares of a 3-of-5 scheme? **Correct:** secrecy needs uniformly random coefficients; a tiny support leaves few candidate secrets. Distractors: the field becomes too small; the shares become identical; the Lagrange coefficients stop existing.

**`zero-knowledge-proofs` · Zero-Knowledge Proofs** **[domain review]** · **defensive and payload-free, full review before merge**
Track `sec` · *"Prove you know it without revealing it"* · Builds on: `modular-arithmetic`, `hashing-encryption`, `probability-basics` · priority nice
Fences quoted. `hashing-encryption` keyIdea 4: *"Signatures run the other way: sign with the private key, verify with the public key."* and `digital-signatures-pki` own signatures; `diffie-hellman` owns the discrete-log setting in a key exchange. This lesson owns three properties of an **interactive proof**: **completeness** (an honest prover always convinces), **soundness** (a prover without the secret rarely does) and **zero knowledge** (the transcript could have been made without the secret). SNARK and STARK systems are **named only** (`Provenance not-run`, "shown, not run"); the page never claims one is demonstrated. The parameters are toys and the page never says they are secure.

**Spec.**
- **Scene 1 · The cave, as verifier and as cheater.** The Ali Baba cave: a ring with a door in the middle; the prover enters, the verifier (who cannot see) calls "come out of the left path" or "the right path", and a prover who knows the door's passphrase can always comply. A prover who does **not** know it walked in one side and can comply only if the call happens to be that side. Three roles, switchable (`Segmented`):
  - **You are the verifier (default).** A seeded prover who may or may not know the passphrase answers your challenges; you choose each round's side (buttons) or press "random"; after n rounds a confidence meter reads `1 - 2^-n` (0.5, 0.96875, 0.9990234375 for n = 1, 5, 10) and you press **Accept** or **Reject**; the page reveals whether the prover knew the secret and whether you were right. **The wrong move:** if your calls follow a pattern (always left, strictly alternating), a cheating prover that predicts the pattern passes every round and the meter still reads 99.9% at n = 10: the page prints "the meter assumes your challenges are unpredictable; yours were not, and this prover cheated and passed 10 of 10".
  - **You are the cheater.** You choose which side to enter; a seeded verifier calls a random side; you are caught in the first round where it differs. Over many seeded runs the survival of n rounds is `2^-n`: **1,000 seeded games of 5 rounds** survive a number within 3 sigma of `1000 / 32 = 31.25` (sigma = `sqrt(1000 x (1/32)(31/32)) = 5.5`), printed.
  - Nothing is scripted: the prover's side, the verifier's calls and the cheater's guesses come from the seeded generator at play time.
- **Scene 2 · A real proof of knowledge of a discrete logarithm (Schnorr identification, toy group).** Groups (chip `toy`, bit sizes shown): **A** `p = 23, q = 11, g = 2` (5 bits) and **B** `p = 2039, q = 1019, g = 4` (11 bits). The tests verify `p` and `q` prime, `p = 2q + 1`, `g != 1` and `g^q = 1 mod p` (so `g` has order `q`: for A, `2^11 = 2048 = 89 x 23 + 1`; for B `4 = 2^2` and `4^1019 = 2^2038 = 1` by Fermat). The prover's secret `x` (stepper `1..q-1`) gives the public `y = g^x mod p`. One round: the prover picks `r` (seeded) and sends `t = g^r`; the verifier sends a challenge `c` in `0..q-1` (slider, or random); the prover answers `s = r + c x mod q`; the verifier accepts iff `g^s = t y^c mod p`. Every modular power is shown with its arithmetic in a `ConsolePanel`.
  - **[hand] vectors (group A, `x = 7`, `y = 2^7 = 128 = 128 - 115 = 13`):** `r = 5`: `t = 2^5 = 32 = 9`. `c = 4`: `s = 5 + 28 = 33 = 0`; check `g^0 = 1` and `t y^4 = 9 x 13^4`; `13^2 = 169 = 8`, `13^4 = 64 = 18`, `9 x 18 = 162 = 1`: both sides 1, **accept**. `c = 3`: `s = 5 + 21 = 26 = 4`; `g^4 = 16`; `13^3 = 13 x 8 = 104 = 12`; `9 x 12 = 108 = 16`: **accept**. **Extractor:** the two accepting transcripts `(t = 9; c = 4, s = 0)` and `(t = 9; c = 3, s = 4)` give `x = (s - s')/(c - c') = (0 - 4)/(4 - 3) = -4 = 7 mod 11`: the secret. Group B [proto]: `x = 100` gives `y = 1153`; `r = 77` gives `t = 360`; for `c = 500`, `s = 77 + 50,000 mod 1019 = 146` and both sides are 450.
  - **Cheat panel (you do not know `x`).** (a) *Guess the challenge:* choose a challenge `c'` first, then `s` at random and send `t = g^s y^(-c')`; this passes **iff the verifier's `c` equals `c'`**. Over 1,000 seeded rounds the pass count is within 3 sigma of `1000/q` (about 91 of 1,000 for group A, about 1 for group B), printed with the formula `1/q`. (b) *Predictable verifier:* **if the verifier always picks `c = 0`**, any `t = g^s` passes: the page says why, and the soundness bar drops to "no protection". (c) *Search after seeing `c`:* a button tries every `s` in `0..q-1` and **succeeds instantly in these toy groups** ("a toy's discrete logarithm takes at most 11 or 1019 tries; that is exactly why the toy proves nothing"); it is offered only for these two groups and refuses anything larger. Beside it the **cost-of-breaking line**: for a group of order about `2^256` the best generic method is about `sqrt(q)`, about `2^128` steps, about `1e19` years at an illustrative `10^12` steps per second (computed; chip `illustrative`).
- **Scene 3 · Soundness and zero knowledge, made checkable.** *Soundness, as an extractor:* the learner runs the prover **twice with the same commitment `t`** (a "rewind" button: both answers come from the same `r`) with two different challenges, and the page recovers `x` from `(s - s')/(c - c')`. Label: "a proof technique: if a prover can answer two challenges for one commitment, it must know the secret; a real verifier never allows two answers to one commitment". *Zero knowledge, by exhaustion:* the page lists **every** `(t, c, s)` the real prover can produce for group A (each `r` in 0..10 and `c` in 0..10 gives one triple: **121** triples) and **every** triple the **simulator** produces **without the secret** (pick `s` and `c`, set `t = g^s y^(-c)`: **121** triples), and shows the two sets are **identical, each triple exactly once**. A "spot the fake" row shows six transcripts mixed from both sources; every one passes the verifier's equation and the learner cannot tell them apart; a reveal shows the source. The theorem is a `ClaimLabel kind="theorem"`: "honest-verifier zero knowledge: argued, not run beyond this group; a verifier that picks challenges adaptively is not covered (named, not shown)". For group B the equality is asserted in the test over all `1,019 x 1,019 = 1,038,361` pairs (marked slow).
- **Scene 4 · One message, no interaction (Fiat-Shamir) and where SNARKs fit.** The challenge is replaced by `c = SHA-256("kinetic-zk|" + p + "|" + g + "|" + y + "|" + t) mod q` (`core/sha256`, labelled toy). The proof is `(t, s)`; anyone recomputes `c` and checks. **[hand + node] group A, `x = 7`, `r = 5`:** `t = 9`, `c = SHA-256("kinetic-zk|23|2|13|9") mod 11 = 9` [node], `s = 5 + 9 x 7 = 68 = 2 mod 11`; check `g^2 = 4`; `13^9`: `13^2 = 8`, `13^4 = 18`, `13^8 = 324 = 2`, `13^9 = 26 = 3`; `9 x 3 = 27 = 4`: both sides 4, **accept**. Group B [proto]: `x = 100, r = 77`: `y = 1153`, `t = 360`, `c = 609`, `s = 856`, both sides 1251. **Changing `t` after the fact** (the learner edits it) makes the recomputed `c` differ and the check fail. A short panel names zk-SNARKs and zk-STARKs (`not-run`): proofs of arbitrary statements that are short and fast to verify; nothing is run or claimed to be demonstrated; "weak Fiat-Shamir" mistakes (a hash that omits the statement) are named only.

**Out of scope.** Signatures built from Schnorr (`digital-signatures-pki`), commitments and range proofs (named), SNARK/STARK internals, circuits, trusted setups (named, not-run), malicious-verifier zero knowledge and parallel composition (one sentence), any real group or curve.
**Kit.** `core/numberTheory` (`modPow`, `modInverse`, `isProbablePrime`), `core/field` (K4-6, `Fp` for the exponent arithmetic mod q; no new kit), `core/sha256`, `core/random`, `SeedField`, `Segmented`, `Slider`, `ChipButton`, `ConsolePanel`, `CellGrid` (the 11 x 11 triple grids for the exhaustion), `Bar` (confidence), `Stat`, `ClaimLabel` (K4-8 theorem), `Provenance`, `useScenePicker`. Local `cave.ts`, `schnorr.ts`, `transcripts.ts`.

- **Misconception:** "a zero-knowledge proof can be checked by anyone with no interaction in the cave" and "the confidence meter is a fact about the prover". Quiz 1 and 2.

**Quiz** (5 questions; misconception questions 1 and 2).
1. *(misconception)* The verifier in the cave always calls "left". The prover who does not know the passphrase always enters the left path. After ten rounds what does the 99.9% meter mean? **Correct:** nothing: the meter assumes unpredictable challenges, and these were predictable, so a cheater passed. Distractors: the prover almost certainly knows the secret; the prover passed by luck; the meter is wrong because ten rounds is too few.
2. *(misconception)* What does "zero knowledge" claim about the transcript? **Correct:** a simulator without the secret could have produced transcripts with the same distribution. Distractors: the verifier learns the secret only if it asks twice; the transcript is encrypted with the secret; the transcript is never needed to check the proof. Note: distractor 1 contains a half-truth (two answers for one commitment reveal it), which is why it tempts.
3. What is the chance that a prover without `x` passes one Schnorr round in group A (`q = 11`)? **Correct:** 1 in 11, the chance of guessing the verifier's challenge. Distractors: 1 in 2; 1 in 23; 0, because the equation cannot be satisfied.
4. Why does the extractor need two answers to one commitment? **Correct:** two answers differ by `(c - c') x`, so the secret is their difference over the difference of challenges.
